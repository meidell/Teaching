The project

Pick a problem in your city. Use AI to understand it and to find a fix. Show how you did it.

Four steps, five pieces of work

Each step ends with something you hand in

  1. PICK — choose the problem and show how big it is → essay 1, 1,500 words
  2. INVESTIGATE — find the data and the science behind it → essay 2, 1,500 words
  3. PROPOSE — compare solutions and recommend one → essay 3, 1,500 words
  4. PITCH — join the three essays into one report of 4,500 words, and present it in 10 slides
What Sessions 1 and 2 gave you

Three habits that run through the whole project

  • A precise problem: what, where, and one number that shows it. "Fine dust at three school gates in South Delhi" is precise; "pollution in India" is not.
  • A checked source: an AI can invent a study that looks real. Open every link; check every DOI on Crossref (a DOI is the permanent code of a scientific article).
  • A written record: every AI use goes into the Journal: the prompt, the answer, how you checked it, what you kept.
The one idea of Session 3

AI costs water and energy, and AI can also save lives

The cost

Training GPT-3 is estimated to have used about 5.4 million litres of water, counting the power plants. Using it: about one 500 ml bottle for 10–50 answers.

The benefit

Google's AI flood forecasts warn up to 5 days ahead, even on rivers with no measuring station. By Nov 2024 they covered 100 countries where about 700 million people live.

Li, Yang, Islam & Ren (2025), Communications of the ACM 68(7) · Nearing et al. (2024), Nature 627 · Google, Nov 2024.

Before you hand in essay 1

Check these six things

  • The problem names a place and the people affected
  • Three numbers or more, each with source, year and unit, opened and checked by you
  • The text is written by you; AI may comment on a draft, it does not write the essay
  • Every reference has a link or DOI that opens
  • The word count is on the first page
  • The Journal entries for Phase 1 are printed at the end, as the appendix
How to hand in

One PDF, uploaded on the course's hand-in page

  1. Save the essay with the Journal appendix as one PDF
  2. Name it Surname_GreenAI_Phase1.pdf
  3. Open the course page → Hand in → Phase 1, sign in, and upload it
  4. Wait for "Received" with the date and time; take a screenshot

You can upload again before the deadline; the newest file counts. If the upload fails, send the PDF through the 💬 message panel on the course page before 23:59 CET.

After the winter break · essays 2 and 3

Essay 2 investigates the cause; essay 3 compares solutions

Essay 2 · INVESTIGATE · Fri 8 Jan

  • Your question, and the data that answer it
  • At least one table or chart for your city
  • The science behind the problem, and the data's limits
  • One place where an AI was wrong, and how you found out

Essay 3 · PROPOSE · Fri 29 Jan

  • Two or three solutions tried elsewhere, with sources
  • A comparison: six criteria, each scored 0, 1 or 2
  • Your proposal: who does what, what it costs, how to know it works
  • The costs and risks, including the footprint of any AI it uses
February · the final report and the presentation

The three essays, revised, become one report of 4,500 words and a 4-minute talk

  • Report: a new introduction (~300 words) + the three essays, revised with the feedback + a new conclusion (~500 words: challenges, lessons, next steps) + references + the whole Journal
  • Presentation: 10 slides in 3–5 minutes, about 25 seconds a slide: one idea, one picture or number, a few words
  • Both due Sunday 28 February, 23:59 CET; the talk is with your instructor at a time agreed with you
