Why this course exists

Sustainability starts with the place you live, not with the whole planet

The air you breathe on the way to school. The heat you already plan your summer around. The water from the tap. The rubbish that leaves your street. That is where you will work.

Look at one real number · air
178 µg/m³

Delhi's average level of fine dust across the winter of 2024 — about 36 times the World Health Organization's safe yearly guideline of 5 µg/m³.

PM2.5 means dust particles smaller than 2.5 micrometres — so small they pass from your lungs into your blood.

Centre for Science and Environment, winter 2024 · WHO annual guideline, 2021. µg/m³ = micrograms of dust per cubic metre of air.

Look at one real number · water and weather
142 mm in a day

Rain that fell on Dubai on 16 April 2024 — the United Arab Emirates' heaviest since records began in 1949. Roads, homes and the airport flooded.

A city built for almost no rain met a single day of it. That is what a changing climate looks like where you live.

UAE National Center of Meteorology, 2024. mm = millimetres of rainfall.

Look at one real number · waste
160,000 tonnes a day

Household and street waste India's cities throw out every single day. About half is treated; nearly a third is never properly accounted for.

Central Pollution Control Board, India, 2020–21 report under the Solid Waste Management Rules.

Notice what those three facts share

Each one is visible, local and measurable — exactly what your project needs

  • Visible — you can see it, photograph it, or smell it
  • Local — it is in your own city, not "the planet"
  • Measurable — a number describes it, so you can track it

Hold on to these three words. In a few minutes you will use them to choose your own problem.

The project

Pick a real problem in your city, use AI to understand and solve it, and show how

  1. PICK — a real environmental problem you can see and touch
  2. INVESTIGATE — use AI to dig into the data, the science and the local facts
  3. PROPOSE — use AI to explore solutions, and choose one for your city
  4. PITCH — the final report and a short presentation
Why AI sits at the centre of this course

AI tools are fast and fluent — and they are sometimes confidently wrong

What they do well

  • Search and summarise fast
  • Explain hard ideas simply
  • Read and sort data
  • Help you draft and structure

What they get wrong

  • Invent sources that do not exist
  • Make up numbers
  • Sound certain when guessing
  • Miss what is true only in your city

"Show how" means you record every step and check every claim. That is half the project.

What you hand in, and how it is graded

Three short essays grow into one report, plus a short talk

  • Three essays of 1,500 words — one per phase, with feedback each time
  • They join into one final report of 4,500 words — the main graded piece
  • A presentation of 3–5 minutes with 10 slides, to your instructor

Report 25% + presentation 10% = the Applied Project, 35% of your Year 2 grade. (SUMAS CrS Assessment Tree.)

How the three essays become the report

Each essay is a draft of one third of your report

  • Essay 1 · the problem — what it is, where, how big, who it hurts
  • Essay 2 · the investigation — the data and science, checked against real sources
  • Essay 3 · the proposal — solutions tried elsewhere, and the one you recommend
  • Final report — the three, revised with feedback and joined into one argument

You hand in early so you get feedback while there is still time to act on it.

Phase 1 · PICK

The whole project rests on choosing a good problem

Choose well and every later step gets easier. Choose something too big or too vague and you fight it for four months.

What makes a problem good to work on

A good problem is visible, local, measurable, documented and actionable

  • VisibleYou can see it, photograph it or measure it yourself.
  • LocalIt is in your city or neighbourhood — not "the planet".
  • MeasurableA number describes it: µg/m³, tonnes, °C, litres, a count.
  • DocumentedReal data or studies exist for your city or a similar one.
  • ActionableSomeone — a school, a city office, residents — could act on it.
Narrow your problem until it fits in 4,500 words

A topic is too big. A problem is something you can measure.

Too big — a topic

"Air pollution in my city"

Right size — a problem

"PM2.5 at my school gate during the morning drop-off, compared with a street two blocks away"

Narrow is better than broad. The narrow version tells you exactly what to measure and where.

Six places a good problem often hides

Start from what you already walk past

  • Air — dust and traffic fumes near your school
  • Waste — where your rubbish goes, and who pays
  • Water — shortages, flooding, or unequal access
  • Biodiversity — trees, birds or green spaces disappearing
  • Urban heat — districts with no shade that bake in summer
  • Flooding — streets that flood every storm or monsoon

Ask yourself: what do you walk past that nobody is fixing? What did your grandparents have — clean air, a swimmable river — that you do not?

Your first essay, due in early December

The Phase 1 essay must do five things

  • Name the problem, place and people affected, precisely
  • Give three numbers, each with its source, year and unit — and checked by you
  • Explain why it matters: health, money, nature or fairness
  • Say what is already being done, and by whom
  • End with the question Phase 2 will investigate

1,500 words, written in your own words, with the journal as an appendix.

Your proof that the work is yours

Every time you use AI, write down three things

  1. The prompt — exactly what you asked
  2. The output — what the AI gave back
  3. Your reflection — what you checked, and what you kept

This is the AI Process Journal. You hand it in with every essay. It is what makes the work yours, even when AI helped.

Why you check everything

An AI gave one student four sources for Milan's air — two were invented

The student asked for studies on air quality in Milan. Two of the four were real. Two were made up: wrong reference codes, authors who do not exist.

DOI = a web code that points to one exact study. You can paste it into Crossref (a free index of real research) to check the study is genuine.

The lesson: never cite a source an AI gave you until you have opened it and checked it yourself.

The five rules for using AI in this project

Use AI openly, and prove it

  • AI is part of the method — use it to search, explain, draft and check, and record each use
  • The writing is yours — AI may comment on a draft; it does not write the essay
  • Verify every source before you cite it
  • Cross-check what matters against a second tool and a real source
  • No personal data in prompts — not your address, not classmates' names
Do this now · about 5 minutes

Ask one AI for your city's biggest problems — and log it

"What are the three most serious environmental problems in [your city], with a source for each?"
