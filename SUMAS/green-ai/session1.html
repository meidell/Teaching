✅ Block 1

The brief

The Green AI pathway asks one thing: pick a real environmental problem in your own city, use AI tools to understand it and to explore solutions, and show — openly — how you used them. The problem has to be one you can see and touch: the air on your way to school, the rubbish in your neighbourhood, the heat in your district, the river behind the houses.

"Show how" is half of the project. AI assistants are fast and fluent, and they are sometimes wrong. The pathway is not about getting AI to write a report. It is about using four different tools for what each does best, checking what they tell you against real sources, and keeping a record of every step.

✅ Block 3

Three essays, one report

The three essays are not three separate pieces of homework. Each one is a draft of one third of the final report, handed in so that you receive feedback while there is still time to act on it. The final report is the three, revised and joined into one argument.

Phase 1 essay · 1,500The problem: what it is, where, how big, who it hurts, what is already known.
Phase 2 essay · 1,500The investigation: the data and the science, checked against real sources, and what they show about your city.
Phase 3 essay · 1,500The proposal: solutions tried elsewhere, how they were compared, and the one you recommend for your city.
Final report · 4,500 words = the three essays, rewritten with the feedback, with one introduction, one conclusion and one AI Process Journal
What "revised" means
  • Remove what the feedback said was weak or repeated; the final report must still be 4,500 words, so space is precious.
  • Make the three parts point in one direction: the problem in Part 1 is the problem the proposal in Part 3 solves.
  • Update the numbers: if Phase 2 found a better source than the one quoted in Phase 1, Part 1 changes.
  • Write one introduction and one conclusion for the whole report, instead of three.

Keep every version. The journal and the drafts are the evidence of how the work developed, which is exactly what the report is asked to document: the challenges faced, the solutions devised and the lessons learned.

✅ Block 4

Phase 1 · PICK — what makes a good problem

A good project problem has five properties. Each one prevents a specific way the project can fail later.

PropertyThe testWhat goes wrong without it
VisibleYou can see it, photograph it, smell it or measure it yourself.The report stays abstract; you cannot add first-hand evidence.
LocalIt is in your city or neighbourhood, not "the planet"."Climate change" is too large to investigate or solve in 4,500 words.
MeasurableSome number describes it: µg/m³ of PM2.5, tonnes of waste, °C, litres, a count of species.Phase 2 has nothing to analyse.
DocumentedData or studies exist for your city or a similar one.AI tools invent what does not exist; with no real sources, nothing can be checked.
ActionableSomeone — a school, a city office, a company, residents — could do something about it.Phase 3 has no one to propose a solution to.
The questions SUMAS asks you to bring

What do you walk past every day that nobody is fixing? What did your grandparents have — clean air, a swimmable river, a forest behind the house — that you don't? What in your neighbourhood would you not want your future children to grow up with?

Starting points from the pathway's own examples, to be adapted to your city: air pollution (PM2.5 near schools, traffic emissions, smog days per year), waste (what your city throws away, where it goes, who pays), water (drought, groundwater stress, unequal access), biodiversity (species disappearing, green corridors broken), urban heat (hot districts, missing shade), flooding (streets that flood every monsoon or storm).

Narrow is better than broad. "Air pollution in my city" is a topic. "PM2.5 at the gates of my school during the morning drop-off, compared with a street two blocks away" is a problem.

✅ Block 6

The Phase 1 essay — sections and a word budget

The essay is 1,500 words, plus its AI Process Journal as an appendix. A structure that works, with a starting budget you can change:

Five things the Phase 1 essay must do
  1. Name the problem, the place and the people affected, precisely.
  2. Give at least three numbers about it, each with its source, year and unit — every one checked by you, not only by an AI.
  3. Explain why it matters: health, money, nature, fairness.
  4. Say what is already being done, and by whom.
  5. End with the question Phase 2 will investigate.

Write in your own words. References in one consistent style, each with a link or DOI that works. The journal entries you made while writing go at the end.

✅ Block 7

The AI Process Journal — and the rules for using AI

Every AI interaction, for every essay, goes into the journal: the prompt, the output, and your reflection. The journal is handed in as the appendix of each of the three essays and of the final report. It is what makes the work yours, even when AI helped you build it.

Example entry — a student working on air quality in Milan Prompt (to Perplexity): "Find peer-reviewed studies on PM2.5 air quality in Milan, 2018–2024."
Output: four sources. Two were real. Two were fabricated — wrong DOIs, authors who do not exist.
Reflection: two out of four were hallucinated. I checked the DOIs on Crossref. Lesson: never use an AI source without verifying it first.

An entry on this site has eight short fields: date, phase, tool, why you used it, the prompt, what came back, how you checked it, and what you kept. The Journal page stores them, syncs them to your account and prints them as an appendix.

The rules
  • AI is part of the method. Use it to search, brainstorm, structure, read data and check — and record each use.
  • The writing is yours. AI may comment on a draft; it does not write the essay.
  • Every source is verified before you cite it: open the link, check the DOI on Crossref or the title on Google Scholar.
  • Cross-check what matters. An important claim from one AI is checked against a second tool and against a real source.
  • No personal data in prompts: not your address, not classmates' names, not photos of people.
Do this now · 5 minutes

Ask one AI assistant: "What are the three most serious environmental problems in [your city], with a source for each?" Open the Journal and log it as your first entry, including whether the sources exist.
