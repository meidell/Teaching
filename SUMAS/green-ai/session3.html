Green AI: what it costs, and what it can fix

About 90 minutes. Bring your problem and your data cards from Session 2.

Two things are true at the same time. Running AI uses electricity and water. And AI is already saving lives and resources — flood warnings that reach phones days earlier, satellites that spot forests being cut. This session puts numbers on both, shows how one of those systems works, then turns to your Phase 3 question: which solution would you build for your city, and how do you compare it with the others? It ends with the final report and the 10-slide presentation.

Two truths about AI

AI costs the planetTraining GPT-3 in Microsoft's U.S. data centres is estimated to have evaporated about 700,000 litres of fresh water on site, and about 5.4 million litres in total once the water used by power plants to make its electricity is counted — roughly two Olympic pools. For using the model, the same study estimates one 500 ml bottle of water for roughly 10–50 medium-length responses.Li, Yang, Islam & Ren (2025), "Making AI less 'thirsty'", Communications of the ACM 68(7), 54–61, doi:10.1145/3724499.
AI helps the planetGoogle's AI flood forecasts give reliable warnings of extreme river floods up to 5 days ahead in river basins with no measuring station at all. By November 2024 they covered 100 countries where about 700 million people live, with alerts in Search, Maps and phone notifications. A World Bank study estimated that bringing early-warning systems in developing countries up to developed-country standards would save an average of 23,000 lives a year.Nearing et al. (2024), Nature 627, 559–563, doi:10.1038/s41586-024-07145-1 · Google, Nov 2024 · Hallegatte (2012), World Bank Policy Research Working Paper 6058, doi:10.1596/1813-9450-6058.

Your project lives between these two facts. Phase 3 asks what AI could do for your problem; the honest answer includes what that AI would cost.

✅ Block 2

Where the energy and the water go — and why estimates differ

An AI model runs on thousands of specialised chips (GPUs or TPUs) in data centres. Its footprint has two phases and three channels.

PhaseWhat happens
TrainingOnce per model: weeks or months of thousands of chips running day and night to adjust the parameters (Session 2, Block 1).
InferenceEvery time anyone uses it: each answer is computed token by token. Small per prompt, but repeated billions of times a day.
ChannelHow it arises
ElectricityThe chips, plus the cooling and power equipment of the building.
Water on siteMany data centres cool their servers by evaporating water in cooling towers.
Water off site, and carbonPower plants that make the electricity use water for cooling and, if they burn fuel, emit CO₂. This depends on the local electricity grid.

That is why two serious estimates can differ by a factor of 50 or more. Compare the study above with Google's own measurement, published in August 2025, for its Gemini assistant: the median text prompt used 0.24 Wh of electricity and 0.26 ml of water, and emitted 0.03 g of CO₂ equivalent — counting the data centre's overheads but not the training of the model.

✅ Block 3

How AI protects: one system in detail, and four domains

Flood forecasting where there are no gauges

Classic flood forecasts need a stream gauge: a station measuring how much water flows down the river, with years of records to calibrate a model of that one river. Most rivers in the countries most exposed to floods have no gauge. Google's approach, published by Nearing and colleagues in Nature in 2024, works like this:

  1. Training data. Decades of daily river-flow records from thousands of gauged rivers around the world, together with the weather those rivers received and the properties of each basin — area, slope, soil, land cover, climate.
  2. The model. A type of neural network for sequences (an LSTM) learns how rainfall, temperature and basin properties turn into river flow over the following days — a single model for all basins, not one per river.
  3. Prediction in ungauged basins. Because it has learned the relation from basin properties, it can be applied to a river it has never seen measured, fed with weather forecasts.
  4. Testing. The authors tested it on gauged rivers held out of training. Its reliability for extreme events up to 5 days ahead was similar to or better than the existing global system's same-day estimates.
  5. Delivery. Forecasts are published on Flood Hub and pushed as alerts to phones in the areas at risk.

Notice what made it work: a precise problem (river floods), decades of real measurements, a test on data the model had not seen, and a way to reach the people at risk. The same four questions apply to any AI idea in your Phase 3 essay.

The four domains in the pathway

EnergyForecasting wind and solar output so that the grid wastes less power; optimising heating and cooling in large buildings.
LandSatellite images analysed to spot deforestation, methane leaks and illegal fishing in near real time — for example Global Forest Watch's tree-cover-loss alerts and UNEP's satellite-based Methane Alert and Response System.
FoodA farmer photographs a sick plant; an image model identifies the disease and suggests what to do. Less pesticide, less crop lost.
CitiesFlood early warning, traffic-flow optimisation, and cameras with AI that sort waste on recycling lines.

The sector hires data scientists, climate-tech engineers, ESG analysts, product managers and sustainability consultants.

✅ Block 4

Comparing solutions for your city

Phase 3 is not "AI will fix it". It is a comparison: what has been tried elsewhere, what worked, and which option fits your city. Some of the best solutions do not use AI at all; say so if that is what you find. Score two or three candidate solutions from 0 to 2 on six criteria.

CriterionThe question
FitDoes it address the cause your Phase 2 investigation found, in your city?
EvidenceHas it worked somewhere comparable — with a source?
Who actsIs there someone who could start it: the school, the city, a company, residents?
CostIs it affordable for whoever would pay?
SpeedWould it show an effect within a few years?
Footprint and fairnessAre its own costs (energy, water, data, jobs) small, and does it help the people most affected?
