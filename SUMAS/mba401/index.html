About this course

What it's about

Inferential statistics — hypothesis testing and confidence intervals — for a variety of situations, and the beginnings of multivariate statistics: simple and multiple linear regression. We learn each method by applying it to a real decision the pizzeria has to take, so that the statistics of a professional role — a data analyst's, say — is practised, not just heard.

Prerequisites

None. Week 1 builds the descriptive part from scratch and Week 2 opens with the probability rules you need — so if your quantitative background is rusty, the first two weeks are where you fix it.

Use of AI

For the case study you are encouraged to use ChatGPT and other AI tools freely to research, check and refine your work. The final exam: no AI, no computer, no phone. AI helps you learn and prepare; it will not be in the room when you are asked to demonstrate what you know.

What you'll be able to do

These are the course's official learning objectives. Each one is a thing you will be able to do by Week 10 — with the week that teaches it, so you always know where you are on the map.

  • Apply the theory of sampling distributions. Explain why a sample mean from a sample of thirty behaves predictably even when the population does not — the idea every later method rests on. Week 3A
  • Estimate parameters and construct confidence intervals. Turn one sample into a statement of the form "the true value is between here and here, and I am 95% confident" — for a mean, a proportion and a variance. Week 3B
  • Perform statistical tests in a variety of situations. Take a claim, decide what evidence would refute it, compute the evidence, and reach a defensible conclusion — including what a p-value does and does not say. Week 4A
  • Compare two populations, and tell independent from paired samples. Decide whether a difference between two groups is real, and recognise which of the two designs you are looking at — the single most-marked judgement on the exam. Week 4B
  • Apply simple linear regression and compute its coefficients. Fit a line to two variables, interpret its slope in the units of the business, and test whether that slope is real. Week 5
  • Apply multiple linear regression for model selection. Extend the line to several predictors and choose between competing models. Weeks 5 & 7
  • Understand chi-square and F-tests. Handle categories and more than two groups — independence, goodness of fit, and one-way ANOVA. Week 6
  • Understand machine learning and the main estimation methods. See where the statistics you have learned sits inside what industry calls machine learning, and what it adds. Week 7
How each one is practisedEvery objective is met three times: the lesson in Part 1 of the session, the exercises in Part 2 worked with the room, and the homework in your own time. If you can do the exercises without the hint, you have the objective.

The running case

Across all nine weeks we run one business: a pizzeria near Plainpalais, and the very first number it must commit to is the price of a Margherita. Week 1 makes you guess it on instinct and then describes the competition; Week 3 says how sure you can be about "the typical price" from a sample; Week 4 tests whether a difference is real; Week 5 asks what predicts price; Week 6 whether topping preference depends on neighbourhood. By the end that gut number has become a defended interval — a recommendation you could stand behind in front of your partner, your bank, and your customers.

The datasets are small and hand-computable on purpose: the exam is by hand. The practice arena is where you get endless fresh numbers.
