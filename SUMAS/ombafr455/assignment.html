The brief

Your mandate

You are an analyst at Léman Sustainable Capital. The fixed-income desk has been asked to build a CHF 250 million labelled-bond sleeve, and the investment committee wants a corporate anchor holding — something large, liquid and defensible in front of a client who reads the ESG press.

Someone has proposed Apple's green bond programme. It is the obvious candidate: enormous issuer, impeccable credit, a mature and well-reported green framework. It is also the kind of holding that gets a firm accused of buying a label. Your case study is the programme's pivotal issue — the $1.0bn second green bond of June 2017 — and your recommendation is about the bond the desk could buy today, the euro tranche 0.500% due 2031.

Write the analyst note. The committee wants a recommendation, not an appreciation.

The question you must answer, in one sentence: on the evidence of Apple's 2017 green bond and the programme around it, should Léman's labelled-bond sleeve hold Apple green bonds today — and if so, what is the sleeve actually buying that an ordinary Apple bond would not deliver?
The case

The programme, as it stands

The facts below were established in Week 5 §5.5. Everything else you need is in the primary sources.

IssueSizeContext
February 2016$1.5bnThe largest green bond by a US corporate at the time
June 2017$1.0bnIssued weeks after the US announced its intention to leave the Paris Agreement — deliberately timed as a signal
November 2019€2.0bnTwo euro tranches (0.000% due 2025, 0.500% due 2031), taking the programme to ~$4.7bn
AssuranceAnnual Green Bond Impact Report · second-party opinion from Sustainalytics · use-of-proceeds examination by Ernst & Young

Eligible categories span low-carbon design and engineering, energy efficiency, renewable energy, carbon mitigation and carbon sequestration. Reported impact runs to hundreds of megawatts of installed renewable capacity and multi-million-tonne lifetime CO₂e reductions.

Which bond is "the case"? Read this before you plan the paper. The syllabus names Apple's second green bond — the $1.0bn issue of June 2017 — and that is the analytical core of this assignment. It is the issue that makes the timing question in Section 3 work at all, and Sections 1 to 4 should be anchored on it, with the 2016 and 2019 issues as context for how the programme developed. The recommendation in Section 6 is different: an investment committee cannot buy a bond retrospectively, so the forward-looking decision concerns the instrument Léman could actually hold today — the euro tranche 0.500% due 2031 from the November 2019 issue. In short: analyse 2017, decide on 2031. The 0.000% euro tranche matured in 2025, so any pricing discussion is about the 2031 bond.
What to write

Six sections — and the weeks each one needs

Write it as an analyst note to an investment committee: a recommendation box at the top, numbered sections, tables where they carry the argument. Word counts are guidance; the total is what is marked.

1 · The instrument~300 wordsWeek 5

What this bond is, in the vocabulary of the course.

  • Place it on the four structures. Test it against the four ICMA Green Bond Principles components one by one — use of proceeds, project evaluation and selection, management of proceeds, reporting — and say whether each is met.
  • Why use-of-proceeds and not a sustainability-linked bond? What does Apple's project mix make possible that Rhône Cement's does not? This is the comparison that shows whether you understand the two designs or have only memorised them.
2 · Additionality~450 wordsWeeks 1, 5 & 7

The hard question, and the one most answers dodge.

  • Apple is one of the most cash-rich companies on earth and could fund every eligible project from its balance sheet. What, then, is the bond for?
  • Work through the candidate answers seriously: signalling, supplier leverage, investor diversification, internal discipline — or nothing. Argue for one; do not list all four and decline to choose.
  • The counterfactual. If Apple had never issued a green bond, what would actually be different in the world? Answer that honestly and you have the additionality debate in a paragraph.
3 · Timing as strategy~250 wordsWeeks 1 & 2

The June 2017 issue landed just after the Paris withdrawal announcement. Read that as corporate strategy, not coincidence.

  • What was being bought, and from whom? Name the audience.
  • Does an issue timed for signalling value make the instrument more credible, less credible, or neither? Defend your answer — all three are arguable.
4 · Who checks the promise~450 wordsWeeks 3 & 5

The section where a strong note pulls away from a summary. Four documents, four different jobs.

  • For each of the framework, the Sustainalytics second-party opinion, the EY use-of-proceeds examination and the annual impact report: what does it attest to, who paid for it, and what is the standard of assurance?
  • Then the sentence that matters: what remains unattested? Be specific about the gap between "the money went where we said" and "the world changed as a result".
  • Would the programme satisfy the EU Green Bond Standard, including the 85% Taxonomy-alignment test? If you cannot tell from the public documents, say that — and say what disclosure would settle it.
Method note. Test the issuer against its own stated criteria and quote the framework's words when you do. An assignment that measures Apple against what Apple promised is far stronger than one that measures it against a general feeling about Apple.
5 · The price of the label~300 wordsWeek 5

Sustainability that costs nothing usually delivers nothing. Put a number on it.

  • Apply the greenium break-even method from §5.4 to the 2031 tranche. State your assumptions explicitly — spread, maturity, comparator — and show the arithmetic.
  • Express the answer as what Léman's clients would forgo, in basis points and in francs on a CHF 250m sleeve.
  • Then answer the committee's real question: is that a fee for impact, or a fee for reporting?
6 · Recommendation~250 wordsWeeks 3 & 6

Take the decision. Buy, don't buy, or buy subject to conditions.

  • Say what the holding does to the sleeve's SFDR position, and what you would have to disclose to a client to hold it honestly.
  • Give the conditions: what disclosure, price or framework change would move you from one side to the other.
  • Name the one fact that, if you learned it tomorrow, would reverse your recommendation.
Timing note. Sections 1, 3, 4 and 5 are fully writable once Week 5 is behind you — which it is. Section 2 is sharper after Week 7's treatment of impact and intentionality, and Section 6 uses Week 6's SFDR and stewardship material. There is no reason to leave this until Week 9.
Sources

What separates a distinction

  • The additionality answer is specific to Apple's balance sheet — it could not be copied onto any other issuer.
  • The verification section distinguishes assurance standards, not just assurance providers, and states plainly what nobody has checked.
  • The greenium is quantified with stated assumptions rather than asserted from memory.
  • The recommendation survives its own strongest counter-argument, which appears in the note.

What loses marks every time

  • Describing the programme instead of assessing it. The five questions are not prompts for a summary.
  • Treating a second-party opinion as an audit, or an impact report as verified outcome data.
  • Concluding that the bond is "greenwashing" or "credible" without naming which of the four greenwashing patterns from §5.5 you are alleging.
  • No recommendation, or a recommendation with no conditions attached.
