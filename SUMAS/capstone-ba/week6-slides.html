A guess in the chat · a percentage

Of all the wind capacity connected worldwide in 2025, how much was in China?

About 73%: 120.5 GW of about 165 GW. In 2024 it was about 68% (79.8 of 117 GW).

China is not only the largest maker of turbines. It is also by far the largest buyer, and that home market is what lets its manufacturers scale.

GWEC, Global Wind Report 2025 and 14 May 2026 release; the percentages are computed.

The price gap

A Chinese turbine costs 20–50% less

About 20%

cheaper than US and European turbines, on average (BloombergNEF, 2024)

Up to 50%

cheaper "in some cases", with up to three years of deferred payment (WindEurope, May 2024)

+45%

rise in Western turbine prices since 2020, to US$1.0–1.2 m per MW (Wood Mackenzie, Nov 2025)

Why so cheap? Scale at home, integrated supply chains, cheaper steel and labour, and, the EU suspects, state support. The EU is investigating that last point.

What went wrong

Blade and bearing problems on new onshore models (about €1.6 bn of charges in 2023), offshore ramp-up costs, and fixed-price orders signed before costs rose.

The others

Vestas: back to a 5.7% margin in 2025. GE Vernova: blade failures at Dogger Bank, where Eni's Plenitude holds a stake, and at Vineyard Wind in 2024.

Week 4's lesson, one level down

The squeeze travels down the chain

Auctions pushed prices down → developers pushed turbine prices down → turbine makers rushed bigger models to market below cost → quality problems and losses → fewer, more expensive Western suppliers.

A balancing loop for your report §5: a lower auction price weakens the supply chain that delivers the next auction.

Ships and cables

Who can install it, and who can connect it

Installation vessels, to 2030

China's fleet can install about 225 GW. Outside China about 175 GW, if €2.5–4 bn a year goes into new vessels for three years, and about 140 GW if it does not. 500 GW is "out of reach".

Kühne Climate Center, January 2026: vessel capacity, not a forecast.

Subsea power cables

Three firms, NKT, Prysmian (Italian) and Nexans, hold about 75% of the high-voltage market, booked into the late 2020s. Europe may be short by up to 27% a year in cables above 132 kV, about 22,800 km over 2026–2040.

FT via Offshore Magazine; TGS | 4C, June 2026.

Sorting · type C or E

Where does Europe depend most on China?

  1. The magnets in a direct-drive offshore turbine. China: 94% of sintered magnets
  2. High-voltage subsea cable. Europe: NKT, Prysmian, Nexans
  3. Castings and converters. China: about 82% each
  4. The offshore turbines installed in Europe so far. Europe: Siemens Gamesa, Vestas

Europe still builds most of its own offshore turbines. The dependency sits one or two levels below them.

Italy · Chinese turbines at sea

The first Chinese turbines in European waters

Beleolico, Taranto, 2022

30 MW, ten Mingyang 3 MW turbines, built by Renexia (Toto group). The first offshore wind farm in the Mediterranean, and the first in Europe with Chinese turbines.

What came next

August 2024: Mingyang, Renexia and the Italian government sign a memorandum for a turbine factory in Italy. October 2024: Mingyang wins the design contract for Med Wind, a 2.8 GW floating farm off Sicily with 18.8 MW turbines.

offshorewind.biz, 2022 and 2024. Whether a factory site has been chosen is not confirmed; check before you cite it.

And doors closing

WhereWhat happened
Germany · Waterkant, 300 MWMingyang named preferred supplier in July 2024; in August 2025 the developer switched to Siemens Gamesa after the Defence Ministry raised security concerns

A modern turbine is connected, monitored and controlled remotely. Buying one is also a decision about who has access to the grid.

The dilemma

Cheap or secure?

Buy Chinese

Turbines 20–50% cheaper, financing offered, faster delivery. Cheaper wind, sooner, at a lower cost to consumers and taxpayers.

Buy European

Jobs and factories at home, no foreign access to control systems, less exposure to export controls. Paid for in a higher price per MWh.

There is no right answer, only a price on security. Your report should say what that price is in your worlds.

Europe's answer

Rules that put a price on resilience

RuleWhat it does
Net-Zero Industry Act (2024)A goal: EU factories cover at least 40% of the EU's needs for clean-energy technology by 2030. From 30 December 2025, auctions must use non-price criteria (cybersecurity, sustainability, resilience) for 30% of volumes, or 6 GW a year per country.
Critical Raw Materials Act (2024)By 2030: 10% of EU needs mined in the EU, 40% processed, 25% recycled, and no more than 65% from any single country
Wind Power Package (October 2023)15 actions to keep the industry European, as wind grows from 204 GW (2022) to more than 500 GW by 2030
Your two companies

Who is exposed to what?

Ørsted

Buys large offshore turbines from European makers. Exposed to their quality problems, their price rises and their capacity, and through magnets to Chinese supply further down. A resilience rule in auctions helps its suppliers, and raises its costs.

Eni / Plenitude

A minority stake in Dogger Bank, where GE's blades failed. A home market, Italy, that has already used Chinese turbines at sea and has an Italian cable champion, Prysmian. Spread across solar and onshore wind, with different supply chains.

For your scenarios

Supply-chain openness is a good axis

Open ↔ weaponised supply chains: a genuine driver, and largely independent of EU climate policy. A strong-policy Europe can still face export controls; a weak-policy Europe can still import cheap turbines.

If your team's axes failed the Week 4 test, this is a candidate replacement. If they passed, it belongs in your worlds as a constraint on each deployment number.
