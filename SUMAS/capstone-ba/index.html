Capstone · AI for Good — European wind to 2040

Ten weeks as an independent sustainability consultant. Your client question: is wind energy a sound long-term investment for Europe's transition to net-zero by 2050 — and which company, Ørsted or TotalEnergies, is better positioned? You build the data-driven, scenario-based analysis that real consulting firms deliver, with AI as your thinking partner, and defend it.

    The course · week by week
    Assessment · how the grade is built

    Discussion forums · on this site

    About a dozen required posts over the term, most with two replies, each marked on evidence, method, judgment and engagement — the same four things every week. Optional forums are read and answered but not graded. Word counts and deadlines are on every forum page.

    Why on the site rather than MoodleSo the brief, the week's tools and the discussion sit on one page — and so I can see on one screen who has posted, who is waiting for a reply, and where the class disagrees.

    Three phase reports · on Moodle

    • Phase 1 — strategic system analysis, 1,500–2,000 words. Stakeholders, loops, two critical uncertainties, four worlds, indicators.
    • Phase 2 — data analysis, 2,000 words + figures. A fitted learning curve, three projections with honest uncertainty bands, an industry cross-check. A ~600-word proposal is approved first.
    • Phase 3 — the integrated consulting report, 4,000–5,000 words. The robust-strategy test, the recommendation, the signals to watch.
    Feedback before the next deadlineYou receive written feedback on each phase before the following one is due, so each report can correct the last. Late penalty 5% per 24 hours.

    Final exam · Week 10 · on Moodle

    60 multiple-choice questions, 2 hours, closed book, one attempt, sequential questions. It tests whether the toolkit transfers to a case you have not seen — a different technology, geography or decision. Week 10 has the revision guide and a practice prompt with a 90-minute timer. Late is zero, no exceptions.

    The standard on AI

    AI is encouraged and required. You must understand and be able to defend every analysis, calculation and conclusion, whether produced alone or with AI. Every hand-in ends with a declaration of which assistants you used and for what. "The AI said so" is never an answer a client accepts — and from Week 3 on, finding where your assistant is wrong is an assignment.
