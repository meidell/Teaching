Three questions after the team presentations

  1. Where do you agree? Find two claims every member of the team would accept, about Ørsted, about Eni, or about the growth of wind.
  2. Where do you contradict each other? Name one tension: where the data lead's numbers clash with someone's scenario, or with a feedback loop.

The context · Raworth (2017)

We chose the wrong goal

Kate Raworth, TED talk, 2018

70+ years

of GDP as the dominant policy goal, since the 1940s. Raworth's image: economics lost sight of its goals, and GDP moved into the empty nest like a cuckoo.

Planetary boundaries crossed

4 of 9 when Raworth wrote; 6 of 9 by 2023 (Richardson et al., Science Advances); 7 of 9 in the 2025 Planetary Health Check, which added ocean acidification.

The Doughnut · a 21st-century compass

Between shortfall and overshoot

The social foundation · inner ringTwelve essentials every person needs: food, water, energy, health, education, housing, income and work, political voice, gender equality, social equity, networks, peace and justice. Below it: shortfall.
The ecological ceiling · outer ringNine planetary boundaries: climate change, biodiversity loss, land conversion, freshwater, ozone layer, ocean acidification, chemical pollution, air pollution, nitrogen and phosphorus. Beyond it: overshoot.

Progress is not an ever-rising GDP line. It is humanity thriving in the safe and just space between the two rings.

Applying the Doughnut

Where European wind sits

Pressure on the ceilingRare earths and steel in turbines · cables and seabed impacts offshore · land use onshore · blades at end of life, not yet solved
Building the foundationAffordable energy for households · a just transition for fossil-fuel workers · energy security for smaller member states · local acceptance of projects
Where Ørsted sitsA business model largely inside the safe space; its supply chain pushes against the ceiling; offshore projects affect marine biodiversity. Is it regenerative by design?
Where Eni sitsA core fossil business that overshoots the ceiling; Plenitude tries to rebalance, but at what pace? Gas and LNG against renewables growth: a direct tension. Can one company straddle both sides?
The key move

From a stakeholder map to a system

1 · InventoryWho are the actors?Ørsted, Eni, the European Commission, grid operators, banks, households, NGOs, regulators. Most Week 1 maps stop here.
2 · InteractionsHow do they influence each other?Regulation → investment → price signals → policy revision → new regulation. Systems analysts need to reach this level.
3 · DynamicsWhat are the feedback loops?Reinforcing: falling LCOE → more investment → more scale → lower LCOE. Balancing: permitting bottlenecks limit build-out despite falling costs. This is where strategy lives.

Raworth: the ecological ceiling is itself a feedback constraint, a balancing loop that caps economic activity whether we design for it or not.

Source quality

Know what your data actually is

Primary data

Company filings (annual reports, CDP), official statistics (Eurostat, Bundesnetzagentur), peer-reviewed datasets.
✓ Traceable, audited or reviewed.
⚠ One or two years' lag; some figures self-reported.

Aggregated statistics

IRENA, IEA, WindEurope, Ember: primary data compiled and cleaned for policy use.
✓ Widely cited by regulators.
⚠ Methods vary; "renewable" is defined differently.

Modelled projections

IEA scenarios, ENTSO-E TYNDP, BloombergNEF outlooks to 2040+.
✓ Essential for foresight.
⚠ Built on assumptions about policy, technology and behaviour: interrogate them.

Before citing a number: is it measured, compiled or modelled? Which year? Which geography? Which definition?
