The brief

One question, two companies

Is wind energy a sound long-term investment for Europe's transition to net-zero by 2050?

Ørsted · Denmark

From DONG Energy, 85% fossil, to the world's largest offshore wind developer. A pure-play bet on renewables.

Eni · Italy

An integrated oil-and-gas major: renewables, retail and e-mobility in Plenitude, minority stakes in offshore wind (Dogger Bank, Vårgrønn). A hedged transition.

The contrast is the point: all-in versus hedged. Last year's cohort studied the same contrast with TotalEnergies — say so, and say that the showcase report is on the hub and their report page follows its structure. Then the warning: Eni is not TotalEnergies; a team that copies last year's Eni-shaped hole with TotalEnergies' numbers will be marked down. Compare Plenitude, not the group.
The brief

What the report has to answer

  • Will wind dominate Europe's electricity mix by 2040?
  • Which company is better positioned — Ørsted or Eni?
  • What are the key risks, uncertainties and opportunities?
  • Under what scenarios does each strategy succeed or fail?

A professional consulting report, backed by data, scenarios and systems analysis. One per team. Written on this site.

Read the four aloud, slowly. Everything for ten weeks is evidence for or against these. The fourth bullet is the one first-year analysts skip: "it depends" with no conditions is not an answer.

How the course works

What "open book" means — and does not

Allowed

Your notes · the readings · this site and the glossary · your team's report and your own consulting file · pen, paper, a simple calculator.

Not allowed

AI assistants of any kind · classmates — your team is not in the room · translation tools.

Sixty questions in two hours is two minutes each. The notes can check a definition; they cannot make the move for you.

Say it once, clearly, and say it again in Week 10. The questions are transfer questions — a new technology, geography or decision — so memorising the Ørsted numbers helps nobody; knowing which tool to reach for does.

AI

Required — and audited

  • You use Claude, ChatGPT or Perplexity every week. You document it.
  • You verify its output against primary sources. From Week 3, catching it being wrong is an assignment.
  • You estimate what it costs in energy and water (Weeks 6–7).
  • You must understand and be able to defend every analysis, calculation and conclusion — whether you, the AI or a teammate produced it.

"The AI said so" is never an answer a client accepts. Neither is "my teammate wrote that part".

This is the course's standard and the charter's third question. Ask each team what its AI policy says so far. The declaration at the end of every phase is section 18 of the report.
