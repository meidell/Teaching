The two questions, asked from opposite ends

Sensitivity asks: of everything that really was fraud, what share did we catch? It is computed down the 'actually fraud' row — 180 caught out of 200, so 90%. You will also hear it called recall, or the true-positive rate.

Specificity asks the mirror question: of everything that really was fine, what share did we correctly leave alone? 97,804 out of 99,800, so 98%. A 2% false-alarm rate is the same statement upside down.

Your own success criteria, renamed

Every student on this course has written a version of "catch at least 90% of fraud, keep false alarms under 2%". Those are not two arbitrary targets. They are sensitivity ≥ 0.90 and specificity ≥ 0.98, and naming them properly is what makes them testable at a stage gate.

Now the sting. Precision asks a third question the other two cannot: of the alerts we actually raised, what share were real? That is 180 out of 2,176 — 8.3%. Both targets are met in full, and more than nine in ten alerts are still false.

The threshold is a dial, and it is yours

Sensitivity and specificity trade against each other through a single setting: how confident the model must be before it calls something fraud. Turn it down and you catch more fraud and block more innocent customers. Turn it up and the reverse.

There is no technically correct position on that dial. Where it sits is a business decision about which error you would rather make — which makes it a project manager's decision, not a modelling one.

What you should be able to say afterwards
Takeaways
  • Sensitivity = of the real positives, how many did we catch. Recall is the same thing under a different name.
  • Specificity = of the real negatives, how many did we leave alone. "False alarm rate under 2%" is specificity above 98%.
  • Precision = of the alerts we raised, how many were real. This is the one that determines whether your operations team can survive the system, and the one nobody puts in the objectives.
  • The threshold is a management decision. Do not let it be set silently inside a notebook.
