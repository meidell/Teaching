Session 3 · resources & budget

What will it actually cost?

Session 3 says the dominant cost driver is rarely the GPU. This is where you find out whether that is true of your project. Put your own numbers in; the model does the arithmetic, shows you where the money goes, and tells you which of your assumptions is doing the most work. The output is a budget you can defend — which is a different thing from a budget you can guess.

About the default rates. Every rate below is an editable, order-of-magnitude teaching default, not a quote. Cloud list prices move constantly, vary by region and are heavily discounted in practice; salaries vary by market. Before this budget goes anywhere real, replace each rate with a number you can source. The model does no currency conversion — cloud prices are usually quoted in USD and salaries in your local currency, so pick one currency and set every rate in it.

3 · Training compute

Accelerators are billed by wall-clock hour, whether or not they are computing. That is why utilisation belongs in a budget and not just in an engineering retro.

4 · Data & storage

Storage is cheap and almost never the problem. Egress — moving data out — is the line that surprises people, and the pipeline that feeds the accelerator is usually where the real engineering cost hides.

5 · Serving the model

Training is a bounded cost. Serving is a small number multiplied by an unbounded one, forever — which is why it usually dominates over a model's life, and why boards that approve a build budget are surprised a year later.
