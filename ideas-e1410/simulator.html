      How this model works — read this, it matters

      A simulation you cannot inspect teaches superstition, so here is the whole thing. It is a deliberately simple set of rules, chosen because each one corresponds to something Sessions 5 and 6 claim. It is not a prediction of your project.

      • Drift. Precision falls about 0.3pp a week as the live population moves away from the training set. With the data shock on, an upstream change at week 18 triples that for six weeks.
      • Detection. Alerting notices within a week of crossing your threshold. Dashboards notice about four weeks later — someone has to look. With no monitoring, nobody notices until the business does, which happens when performance has fallen ten points, and then it costs you far more trust.
      • Retraining. A retrain recovers most, not all, of the gap to the original performance. Without a validation gate, roughly one retrain in five ships a model that is worse — and you find out in production.
      • Adoption. A recall-favouring threshold produces more false positives, so users override more; sustained high override rates collapse adoption. Advisory-only rollout and letting the team set the threshold buy tolerance.
      • Trust. Starts at 60. Communication cadence adds a little every week. Discovering a problem yourself costs a little; the business discovering it for you costs a lot. Delivering visible value adds.
      • Value. Accrues weekly as performance × adoption. Cost accrues weekly, plus a lump for each retrain.

      The randomness is seeded from your choices, so the same decisions always give the same year. Change one and the difference you see is caused by that change — not by a dice roll.

      Run it more than once. The first run tells you almost nothing. Run it, change exactly one decision, run it again — that is the experiment. The decision most students are wrong about is monitoring: it feels like an operational detail to be sorted after launch, and it is the one that decides whether anyone still believes you in month nine.
