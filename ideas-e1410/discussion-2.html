Session 2 · breakout discussion

Pin down the requirements

⏱ 10 minutes in your room · one spokesperson reports back

The brief

An Tâm Health is a private hospital group in Ho Chi Minh City — six clinics, about 4,000 outpatient appointments a week. Roughly one appointment in five is a no-show. Empty slots cost money, and they push waiting times out for everyone else.

The COO wants an AI system that predicts, at the moment of booking, how likely a patient is to miss their appointment — so the clinic can overbook sensibly, target its reminder calls, and offer released slots to the waiting list.

You have four months and a team of four. You have three years of booking records across the six clinics — except that two clinics migrated systems eleven months ago, and their older records are in a format nobody has opened since. And "no-show" is recorded by whichever receptionist closes the day: some mark it, some don't.

A fictional organisation, built for this exercise.

Your five questions

Answer as many as you can in ten minutes. If you are running short, make sure you have done 4 and 5 — those are the two we will spend most time on.

  1. Write one functional requirement. One sentence, precise enough that a developer could build against it and a tester could check it.
  2. Write one non-functional requirement — with a number in it. And be ready to say where your number came from.
  3. Write one data requirement. What data, how much, and how labelled.
  4. The COO says: "Just make it accurate enough that the clinic managers trust it." What is wrong with that sentence — and what would you put in its place?
  5. Three months in, the marketing director asks: "While you're at it, could it also flag patients likely to move to another provider?" Evolving requirement, or scope creep? And what exactly do you do next?
If your room finishes early

Which single requirement on this project could you not pin down yet — and which specific person or role would have to be in the room for you to nail it?

How the room works

Pick a spokesperson firstDo it in the first ten seconds, before you start talking about the problem. Otherwise you will spend minute nine deciding.
Write your answers downIn your room's chat or a doc. You will be asked to read them out, and vague beats nothing but not by much.
Argue about the wordingIf everyone agrees immediately, your requirement is probably too vague to disagree with.
Numbers, not adjectives"Fast", "accurate", "fair" and "reliable" are all banned unless a number follows.
