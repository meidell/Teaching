Session 7 · case workshop

The defence

You have spent seven sessions writing about your project for someone who is on your side. In Session 7 you present it to someone who is not — a practitioner who has shipped AI projects and killed a few, plus a rival team whose job is to argue you should not be funded. Ten minutes. This page tells you exactly what happens, exactly how you are judged, and lets you rehearse against the real questions.

The run of play

Ten minutes per project. The chair keeps time strictly, because a defence where the clock slips is a defence where the confident talk longest.

  • 3 min

    The case

    You present. No slide deck required — your board pack on screen is enough, and honestly better. Cover the problem, why AI, what you would build, what it costs, and what success looks like. Not your methodology. Not your literature review.

  • 4 min

    Challenge

    The jury and the red team ask whatever they like. They have your board pack in front of them. They are not trying to be fair; they are trying to find the weak joint, which is what a real steering committee does.

  • 2 min

    Response

    You answer the hardest one properly. Not all of them — one, well. “I don't know, and here is how I would find out by Friday” is a strong answer. Bluffing is the only fatal one.

  • 1 min

    Verdict

    The jury says fund / fund with conditions / do not fund, and names the condition. It carries no grade. It is the thing you will remember.

Who is in the room

The red team is another student group. Each member takes a card and stays in that character for the whole session — the point is not to be nasty, it is that each card sees a different failure. Print this page and cut them out if that is easier.

💷

The CFO

Cares about: the run rate
“You have told me what it costs to build. What does it cost every year afterwards, and who pays that?”
Push on: a build budget with no run rate; contingency with no named risk attached; benefits that are “efficiency” with no number; a three-year total nobody has calculated.
🧑‍🔧

The head of the team it affects

Cares about: their people
“My team has been doing this for eleven years. What happens to them, and who told them?”
Push on: adoption assumed rather than planned; no one from the affected team in the project; “training” as a single line at the end; a threshold chosen without asking the people who live with the false positives.
🛡️

The data protection officer

Cares about: what could go legally wrong
“Whose data is this, what were they told it was for, and what happens when someone asks why they were declined?”
Push on: explainability treated as optional; no lawful basis named; personal data with no retention rule; a bias assessment that is a sentence; cross-border storage nobody checked.
🔬

The sceptical engineer

Cares about: whether it actually works
“Your labels came from humans who disagree with each other. Why do you believe your accuracy number?”
Push on: a metric with no baseline; test data that leaked; a demo on historical data presented as production performance; “we'll retrain” as an answer to everything; no plan for the day it degrades.
♟️

The rival sponsor

Cares about: the same budget
“For that money I could hire four people. Why is this the best use of it?”
Push on: no comparison to the boring alternative; “AI” as the reason rather than the method; a business case that would look identical for any project; benefits that accrue to no named budget holder.
⏱️

The chair

Cares about: the clock and the rules
“That's time. One more question, then the response.”
Your job: keep to the minute, stop pile-ons, make sure every card gets one question in, and force the verdict to name a condition rather than a feeling.

The three you must be able to answer in one sentence

Everything else can be looked up. If any of these takes you more than a sentence, the thinking behind it is not finished.

1

What decision does this change?

Not what it predicts — what someone does differently on Monday because of the prediction. A model that changes no decision is a science project.

2

How will you know it stopped working?

Name the signal, the threshold and the person who sees it. “We'll monitor it” is not an answer; it is the absence of one.

3

What would make you stop?

The condition under which you would recommend killing your own project. A business case with no kill criterion is advocacy, and jurors can smell it.

How the jury scores you

Published in advance, deliberately. A rubric you can see is a rubric you can aim at — and if you can aim at these five, you can run a real steering meeting.

CriterionNot yetDevelopingStrongExceptional
Problem clarity The problem is described in AI terms, or is really a solution looking for one. A real problem, but its size or cost is not established. The problem, who has it, and what it costs today — in the business's own words. All of that, plus why it has not already been solved the boring way.
Evidence & data realism Data is assumed to exist and be usable. Data sources named; quality problems acknowledged in general terms. Specific data, specific defects, and what it costs to fix them. The above plus a cheap test that would prove feasibility before the big spend.
Cost & value A single number with no derivation, or none at all. A build budget with a stated method. Build cost, run rate and dominant driver, with the key assumption named. The above plus what it costs to be wrong about that assumption.
Risk & governance Generic risks; no owners; ethics as a paragraph at the end. Real risks identified and rated. Risks owned, with triggers and first actions; compliance exposure named. The above plus a stated kill criterion the sponsor has agreed.
Handling challenge Defends everything; treats questions as attacks. Answers the easy questions well, deflects the hard one. Goes to the hardest question directly; concedes what is not known. Uses the challenge to improve the case out loud, in real time.
Why we do this. Every other piece of assessment in this course is read by someone who wants you to do well and knows the material. In your organisation, neither of those will be true. Ten minutes in front of somebody who has no stake in your success is worth more than another two thousand words of coursework — and it is the closest thing this course has to the real thing.
