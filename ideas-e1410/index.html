About this course

What it's about

This course equips you with the skills to manage complex AI projects. It covers advanced project-management methodologies, the AI-specific challenges that make these projects different, resource allocation, risk management, and the tools for planning and delivering AI systems that actually reach production.

Built on the official Cengage-aligned curriculum for E1410, personalised with real-world cases from the instructor's practice.

Prerequisites

Recommended but not mandatory: a basic knowledge of AI concepts and tools. Everything else is built up from Session 1.

What you'll be able to do

By the end of the course you will be able to:

  • Understand the principles of project management in AI-specific contexts.
  • Apply advanced project-management techniques to AI projects.
  • Manage resources and mitigate the risks tied to AI deployments.
  • Develop effective timelines and budgets for AI projects.
  • Evaluate AI project outcomes and ensure successful delivery.

Each session pairs a live class with an interactive lesson, a self-check quiz, and a running-project workbook — so the theory is practised, not just heard.

Assessment · how the grade is built

50% · Final quiz — all eight sessions

A multiple-choice exam sat here on the course site, after Session 8. Its scope is the whole course: foundations, planning, resources and risk (Sessions 1–4) and agile & teams, monitoring, the case workshop and the review (Sessions 5–8).

50 questions · 45 seconds each · one attempt. Your paper is drawn for you from a larger bank — six questions from each of the eight sessions plus two at random — so no two papers are the same. You get your percentage the moment you finish.

Your rehearsal is already built into the course: the self-check quiz that closes every session is written in exactly the exam's style — one attempt per question, no going back, every answer explained. Eight of them, ten questions each. Redoing all eight is the preparation.

🃏 Training cards → all 81, in one place

The training cards pool every session's self-check into one deck, with a hint on each card, clickable definitions, points, and an optional 20-second exam timer. Redoing the eight sessions is still the better preparation; the deck is for the last pass.

Those self-checks are not graded, but your scores reach the instructor dashboard — which is how I see, before the exam, which concepts the cohort has not yet landed.

50% · Final — written case study

The recommended route builds all term: develop your running project — the one you fill in each session's workbook — into an in-depth written case study. (The topic-analysis and country-research options remain open if you prefer.)

Each session's workbook seeds one section of it; the final asks you to expand each into researched, critical analysis.

It must be a project of your own — not MekongPay or Delta Assure. Those are the cases we work together, so they leave you nothing to research. No candidate? Session 1's workshop has five starter projects you can adopt.

Your toolkit · size it, check it, run it forward

Six tools that work on your own project, not on a case study. Each one takes what you have written in the session workbooks and does something with it — and each can write its result back into your workbook, so nothing is typed twice.

💰 Session 3 · budget What will it actually cost? Put your own numbers in — people, GPU hours, storage, serving, contingency — and find out which line dominates, what the run rate is after launch, and which single assumption breaks the budget if you are wrong about it. 🧱 Session 2 · scope Is this a WBS you can trust? Type your breakdown in. It numbers it, rolls up the effort, applies the 8/80 rule, spots activities pretending to be deliverables — and lists the work packages AI projects forget every single time. ⚠️ Session 4 · risk The risk register that argues back Plot your risks on the 5×5 matrix, then get asked the questions a steering committee asks: who owns this, what would you see first, and why is every risk on your list a technical one? 🎛️ Sessions 5–6 · delivery Twelve months later Choose your sprint length, threshold, monitoring, retraining cadence and rollout — then watch a year happen to your project. Same decisions always give the same year: this is consequence, not luck. 📋 Any time · your project The board pack Your answers packaged the way a steering committee receives a proposal — one-page executive summary, one-page canvas, the full case behind them. Print it to PDF and it is a document you could take into your own organisation. ⚖️ Session 7 · the defence Ten minutes in front of someone who is not on your side Exactly what happens, who is in the room, how the jury scores you — plus a rehearsal timer that fires real questions built from your own workbook answers. Sixty seconds each, out loud.

Foundations · video primers

You manage AI projects; you do not have to build the models. But you do have to read what your team reports back — and one accuracy number can hide a system that is quietly unusable. Five short videos from StatQuest (Josh Starmer), each on its own page with the concept explained in project terms first, the video embedded so you never leave the course, and a quick self-check. About 40 minutes for all five. Optional, but Session 5 is far easier if you have done primers 3 and 4.
