Fall Week 2 · Quantitative Methods I · HEG Genève

Cost, revenue, profit — built from parts

Week 1 gave you one function at a time. A business never has one: it has a cost function and a revenue function, and the number the board cares about — profit — is what you get by combining them. This week is the arithmetic of functions: adding, subtracting, multiplying, dividing, composing and inverting.

🏫 In class: 3 h · lesson, then exercises 📖 Operations on functions · composition · inverses ▶ The Lecture button shows the slide outline

By the end of Fall Week 2 you will be able to

  • Combine functions through addition, subtraction, multiplication, division and multiplication by a constant.
  • Identify and use the main families of functions used in business.
  • Build a profit function as P = R − C, and an average-cost function as a quotient.
  • Compose functions — and read the composition as a chain of business steps.
  • Introduce inverse functions and their properties, and say when an inverse exists at all.

Section 2.2 · The four operations

Adding and multiplying functions

Planned · Build

(f+g), (f−g), (f·g), (f/g) and their domains

  • Worked: C(q) = 3 240 + 1.80q and R(q) = 4.50q give P(q) = R − C = 2.70q − 3 240.
  • Widget: pick two functions from a menu and an operation; see the formula, the table and the graph of the result at once.
  • ⚠ The domain of a quotient is the trap: dividing by g kills every x where g(x) = 0. Average cost C(q)/q is undefined at q = 0 — and that is a fact about the business, not a technicality.

Section 2.4 · Composition

A function inside a function

Planned · Chain

(f ∘ g)(x) = f(g(x)) — a chain of business steps

  • Worked: advertising spend → customers → revenue. Two functions chained give one answer, and the chain is the model.
  • Widget: a two-stage machine — feed a number in, watch it pass through g and then f, with the intermediate value shown.
  • ⚠ f(g(x)) and g(f(x)) are different functions. Order is meaning: "10% discount then VAT" is not "VAT then 10% discount".

Section 2.5 · Inverses

Running the machine backwards

Planned · Invert

f⁻¹, when it exists, and what it is for

  • Week 1's supply equation p = q/48 asked for q given p — that is the inverse, and it is the commonest real request.
  • Widget: a function and its inverse drawn together with the line y = x, as the log and exponential were in Week 1.
  • When does an inverse exist? Only if no two inputs share an output — the horizontal line test, and the reason it matters.
