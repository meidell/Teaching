The eleven problems from the printed sheet

These are Homework exercises Week 7 exactly as handed out — same problems, same numbers, same order, including the three review problems at the end. A linear approximation is a function, and a form cannot check a function — so where the sheet asks for one, the fields ask for its slope and intercept, or its value at a stated point. For Newton's method the fields ask for the first iterate and the final answer: if your first step is right, the rest follows. Work each one by hand first, type your answer, and check it. Stuck? Take a hint. Then read the full solution — that is where the learning is.

Round to three decimals unless told otherwise. Use the calculator you will sit the exam with: a Casio FX-82 Solar or a TI-30 eco RS. Getting fluent with its keys now is worth marks in January.

📌 How this homework is marked — it isn't

Homework in this course does not count toward your grade. Neither does the weekly review quiz. They exist so that you, and your instructor, find out what has not landed before the midterm in Week 9 — two weeks from now — at a moment when it is still free to fix.

What does count: 20% fall midterm (MCQ, 60 min) · 40% fall exam · 40% spring exam. Both finals are 120 minutes, closed book, open questions — one calculator and two hand-written sheets recto/verso. Everything you do here is rehearsal for that room.
