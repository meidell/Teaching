# The writing standard — every word a student reads

Jan Erik's standing instruction (3–4 Nov 2026) for all course pages, slides,
exercise instructions, canvas boxes, captions, glossary entries and quiz
questions, in English and in French. The readers are BBA and MBA business
students; many read in their second language. This file is read at the start
of every session; edit it freely.

1. **Define a named idea where it is used, in one plain sentence, before saying
   anything about it.** "The cone of uncertainty — the further out, the wider
   the range" leaves the student guessing what a cone has to do with it. Write:
   "The cone of uncertainty: the range of possible futures is narrow for next
   year and grows wider the further ahead you look, like a cone opening from
   today." Every term (orthogonal, capture rate, LCOE, redispatch…) gets this
   on its first use on a page or slide, even if the glossary has an entry.

2. **Say what the student must DO, with the exact place and the exact thing.**
   Which page, which box, which column, which numbers, what a good answer looks
   like. "Vote on impact and uncertainty" is not an instruction. "Everyone
   types two numbers in the chat, impact 1–5 and uncertainty 1–5; the scribe
   writes the middle value in the table" is.

3. **Say WHY before HOW.** A task opens with the reason it exists in the course
   ("your report needs four scenarios, so…"), in two or three sentences a
   first-year student follows.

4. **No slogans, puns, metaphors, two-beat contrasts, teaser titles or
   punchlines.** A title states the fact or the task in full words ("A wind
   farm is quicker to build than the line it needs"), never a hook ("Europe
   does not lack turbines. It lacks wires.").

5. **No story prop in place of a description.** "Produire le rapport du lundi"
   leaves the reader asking why Monday. Say what the thing is ("le rapport des
   ventes : cinq chiffres sur juillet–octobre") before using the case's name
   for it. A score ("5 sur 5") is named together with what it counts.

6. **Every number is specific:** its unit, its currency (euros for the
   exercises; a source in pounds or dollars is converted at a stated rate), its
   baseline ("higher than the $127 baseline by"), its source and its year.
   "Change vs £95" with no explanation of the £95 is not allowed.

7. **No minute-by-minute timings on student pages.** They stress the students.
   Numbered steps instead. The instructor's own deck keeps its clock.

8. **Short sentences, one idea each; the concrete word before the abstract
   one** ("the line that carries the power", then "(transmission)" once).

9. **Every sentence has a verb. No strings of nouns joined by dots, colons
   or arrows** (Jan Erik, 5 Nov 2026: "always use verbs!"). This applies to
   labels, field captions, placeholders and table notes too. "Platform ·
   followers · median" is not an instruction. "Write which platform the
   account is on, how many followers it has, and what the median is" is.

**Before shipping anything:** read every title and every instruction as a
nineteen-year-old who has never heard the term, and ask "do I know what this
is, and what I have to do?" If not, rewrite.
