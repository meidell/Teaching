Session 5 · Step 1

A percentile says how many values lie below a point, and the distribution shows how many cases take each value

Percentile

The 90th percentile (P90) is the value that 90 % of the cases do not exceed. P25 and P75 mark where the middle half of the cases lies.

At ÒRBITA in October: P25 = 2 rides, P75 = 17 rides, P90 = 33 rides.

Distribution

The whole shape: how many accounts rode 0 to 4 times, 5 to 9 times, and so on. A summary number is chosen after looking at it, never before.

A distribution is skewed to the right when a long tail of large values sits on the right.

Rides, money, ticket times and order sizes are almost always skewed to the right. So the first question about any average in a board pack is: what does the distribution look like?

P25/P75/P90 here are the page's values for all 44,600 paying accounts, paused accounts included.
Session 5 · Step 1

A rate can be split by segment or by cohort, and a total rate can move only because the mix of groups moves

Segment

A group of accounts that share a property: a city, a plan. Answers "where is churn high?"

Cohort

The accounts that joined in the same month. Answers "do the subscribers we win now stay as long as those we won before?" Tenure = months since joining.

Mix

The share of each group in the total. The total rate is the groups' rates weighted by the mix.

city churn = Σ (share of the group × churn of the group)

New subscribers always leave more often than old ones. So a city that wins many new subscribers sees its churn rise even if nobody behaves differently. When the split and the total point in opposite directions, statisticians call it Simpson's paradox.

Simpson, E. H. (1951), "The interpretation of interaction in contingency tables", JRSS B 13(2). Step 1 ends with a sort of six questions into distribution, segment and cohort.

Which number describes a typical paying account, and what does the first bar hide?
The median, 7 rides. 65.7 % of the accounts rode fewer times than the mean. The first bar holds 7,884 accounts with no ride: 4,532 are paused, and 3,352 are active accounts that paid and did not ride once. The 10 % who rode most made 39 % of the 555,552 rides.
