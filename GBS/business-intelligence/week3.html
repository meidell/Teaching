KPIs that survive contact

Group work, self-run: your team works through this page on its own; the instructor walks around and helps. Bring a laptop per team, signed in to this site.

The mandate. ÒRBITA's CFO writes: "The board was told churn is 2.6 %. Our retention budget was set on 4.1 %. Before the next board meeting I need one definition of churn we all use, the few KPIs that tell us whether retention is working, and a contract for each of them that both our systems can compute. One page, please."

Your deliverable · handed in on this page before the quiz (Step 6)

A one-page KPI memo to ÒRBITA's CFO, written as a team: 1. the churn definition you recommend, and the rate it gives · 2. your metric tree · 3. the KPI contract — three KPIs, eight fields each · 4. your recommendation to the CFO in five sentences or fewer. Why it is hard: the sealed decision needs a number. This session shows why "a number" is the hardest part: the same thousand ÒRBITA accounts give a monthly churn of 2.0 percent or 16.1 percent depending on five definition choices nobody wrote down. Teams then build a metric tree from strategic priority down to something a city manager can move by Friday, write the definitions as contracts that both of ÒRBITA's systems can compute, and defend them against another team. Leading versus lagging, and what Goodhart's law does to a bonus scheme.

  1. Each team member, on their own laptop: switch A–E on and off, and watch the rate move. Try Finance's switches and Billing's switches to see the two official figures.
  2. Set the switches to the definition you think is right, write it as one sentence in the first box, and press Put my switches on the wall. Then Show the room to see how the class chose.

This is the mechanism behind every KPI: a definition is a set of filters on a grain. "Churn" is a count of rows that pass a WHERE clause, divided by a count of rows that pass another, and each switch above is one line of that clause. Session 3 puts the rows in a table built so the clause can be written once; the definition has to exist before the table does.

A team field: one member writes it, every member sees it. Name the switches you chose and why, and the rate from the machine. Max. 500 characters.

The class will not agree, and the spread is the lesson: a KPI without a written definition is a different number on every desk.

✅ Step 4 · 15 min · reading

Read: the KPI contract

Do this: each member reads this step (about 10 minutes), sorts the six metrics under "Leading · Lagging · Vanity", and moves the Goodhart slider once from 0 to 1. The step is done when all six metrics are sorted correctly. You need the eight fields for Step 5.

A KPI is not a name; it is a contract with eight fields, and a KPI missing any of them is the dead dashboard from Session 1 waiting to happen. The eight: name · formula · source · grain · owner · cadence · target · counter-metric. The formula is the switches from Step 2 written as a clause. The grain says what one row is before it is counted. The owner is a person, the cadence is the decision's cadence from the sealed sentence, and the counter-metric is the number that goes wrong when this one is gamed.

Leading, lagging, vanity

A lagging KPI records an outcome after it has happened: churn, revenue, retention at ninety days. It is what the board cares about and it cannot be moved this week. A leading KPI moves before the outcome and predicts it: rides in the first fourteen days, time to first ticket resolution. It is what the city manager can act on by Friday. A vanity metric moves, looks good, and drives no decision: app installs, page views, total rides ever. A tree has lagging numbers at the top, leading ones in the middle, and inputs at the bottom; a dashboard with only lagging numbers reports the past.

Leading · Lagging · Vanity

Goodhart's law, with the knob

"When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure." ÒRBITA's support desk is paid a bonus on tickets closed per day. The model below is a toy, stated as one: it assumes agents respond to the bonus weight by closing faster and reopening more. Move the weight and watch the counter-metrics.

The contract's eighth field exists because of this: every KPI that becomes a target needs a counter-metric on the same dashboard, chosen so that gaming the first moves the second. Tickets closed pairs with reopen rate; rides per bike pairs with rides under two minutes; sign-ups pair with ninety-day retention.

Five terms from today

  • Metric tree: priority → outcome KPI → driver KPIs → inputs someone can move by Friday.
  • KPI contract: name, formula, source, grain, owner, cadence, target, counter-metric. A KPI missing one is a report.
  • Leading and lagging: moves before the outcome, or records it afterwards. Both are needed; only one can be acted on.
  • Goodhart's law: a measure that becomes a target stops measuring. The counter-metric is the defence.
  • Definition as a clause: every switch in Step 2 is a line of a WHERE clause over a grain. Session 3 builds the table it runs on.
