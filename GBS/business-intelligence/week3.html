KPIs that survive contact

Bring a laptop, and your team's sealed decision from Session 1.

The sealed decision needs a number. This session shows why "a number" is the hardest part: the same thousand ÒRBITA accounts give a monthly churn of 2.0 percent or 16.1 percent depending on five definition choices nobody wrote down. Teams then build a metric tree from strategic priority down to something a city manager can move by Friday, write the definitions as contracts that both of ÒRBITA's systems can compute, and defend them against another team. Leading versus lagging, and what Goodhart's law does to a bonus scheme.

A random thousand of ÒRBITA's accounts on 1 October: 840 paying, 95 paused, 40 on trial, 25 unpaid for more than thirty days. During October, 41 cancelled: 22 who were paying, 12 who were paused, 3 trials, 4 unpaid. 5 of the paying cancellations were reversed within thirty days. This is the mechanism behind every KPI: a definition is a set of filters on a grain. "Churn" is a count of rows that pass a WHERE clause, divided by a count of rows that pass another, and each switch above is one line of that clause. Session 3 puts the rows in a table built so the clause can be written once; the definition has to exist before the table does.

The room will not agree, and the spread is the lesson: a KPI without a written definition is a different number on every desk.

✅ 17:13 · 20 min · Step 4

The KPI contract

A KPI is not a name; it is a contract with eight fields, and a KPI missing any of them is the dead dashboard from Session 1 waiting to happen. The eight: name · formula · source · grain · owner · cadence · target · counter-metric. The formula is the switches from Step 2 written as a clause. The grain says what one row is before it is counted. The owner is a person, the cadence is the decision's cadence from the sealed sentence, and the counter-metric is the number that goes wrong when this one is gamed.

Leading, lagging, vanity

A lagging KPI records an outcome after it has happened: churn, revenue, retention at ninety days. It is what the board cares about and it cannot be moved this week. A leading KPI moves before the outcome and predicts it: rides in the first fourteen days, time to first ticket resolution. It is what the city manager can act on by Friday. A vanity metric moves, looks good, and drives no decision: app installs, page views, total rides ever. A tree has lagging numbers at the top, leading ones in the middle, and inputs at the bottom; a dashboard with only lagging numbers reports the past.

Leading · Lagging · Vanity

Goodhart's law, with the knob

"When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure." ÒRBITA's support desk is paid a bonus on tickets closed per day. The model below is a toy, stated as one: it assumes agents respond to the bonus weight by closing faster and reopening more. Move the weight and watch the counter-metrics.

The contract's eighth field exists because of this: every KPI that becomes a target needs a counter-metric on the same dashboard, chosen so that gaming the first moves the second. Tickets closed pairs with reopen rate; rides per bike pairs with rides under two minutes; sign-ups pair with ninety-day retention.

Defend the definitions

✅ 18:20 · 10 min · Step 7

The close

Five terms from today

  • Metric tree: priority → outcome KPI → driver KPIs → inputs someone can move by Friday.
  • KPI contract: name, formula, source, grain, owner, cadence, target, counter-metric. A KPI missing one is a report.
  • Leading and lagging: moves before the outcome, or records it afterwards. Both are needed; only one can be acted on.
  • Goodhart's law: a measure that becomes a target stops measuring. The counter-metric is the defence.
  • Definition as a clause: every switch in Step 2 is a line of a WHERE clause over a grain. Session 3 builds the table it runs on.
Break this claim · by next Thursday
