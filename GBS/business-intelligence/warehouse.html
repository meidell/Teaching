The four tables in this warehouse

The main table has one row per account, the grain Session 3 called the "account-month": who the account is, what it did in October, and whether it cancelled. The three small tables are marts: tables the warehouse keeps already counted, for questions asked every month.

w6_accounts_oct

One row per paying account on 1 October 2026 · 44,600 rows

ColumnWhat it holds
account_idThe account's number.
cityBarcelona, Madrid, Lisbon, Milan, Zürich or London.
plan'city-flex' or 'ebike-pro'.
status_on_1_oct'active' or 'paused' (4,532 paused accounts, which pay nothing and cannot ride).
joined_monthThe month the account was opened, for example '2026-07'.
tenure_monthsCompleted months since joining, on 1 October: 1 for September, 3 for July.
tenure_band'1-3', '4-12' or '13+' months: Session 5's three groups of tenure.
promo_1eur1 if the account joined with Barcelona's first month at 1 euro (July to September 2026), else 0.
rides_octThe rides the account took in October.
rides_first_14_daysThe rides in the account's first 14 days. Filled only for accounts in their first 3 months (tenure band '1-3'); empty (NULL) for the others.
cancelled_oct1 if the account cancelled during October, else 0. The churn rate of a group is the average of this column.

w6_churn_month

One row per city and month, May to October 2026 · 36 rows

ColumnWhat it holds
city, monthThe city, and the month as '2026-05' … '2026-10'.
accounts, cancellationsPaying accounts on the 1st of the month, and the cancellations during the month.

w6_tenure_may

May 2026, by city and tenure band · 18 rows

ColumnWhat it holds
city, tenure_band, accounts, cancellationsThe same counts as the main table's, for May, so October can be compared with May.

Part 2 · Exercise 1 · the typical account

Compute the mean and the median of October's rides

Why. The board pack says "12.5 rides per subscriber". Session 5 showed that half of the accounts rode 7 times or fewer. Prove both numbers from the rows.

AVG(x) is the mean; MEDIAN(x) is the middle value once the values are sorted. ROUND(x, 2) keeps two decimals.

Your task

  1. Return one row with two columns: the mean of rides_oct, rounded to 2 decimals, then its median.
  2. Use every account: no WHERE.
SELECT -- the mean, rounded to 2 decimals, then the median FROM w6_accounts_oct;
Exercise 2 · GROUP BY

Compute October's churn for each city, the highest first

Why. The company's 2.62 % is an average of six cities. The CFO needs to see the six.

Churn in % = 100 × cancellations ÷ accounts. Because cancelled_oct is 1 or 0, SUM(cancelled_oct) counts the cancellations and COUNT(*) counts the accounts. Write 100.0, with the decimal point, so the division keeps its decimals.

Your task

  1. Four columns: city, the accounts, the cancellations, the churn in % rounded to 2 decimals.
  2. One row per city, the highest churn first.
SELECT city, COUNT(*) AS accounts -- add the cancellations and the churn in % FROM w6_accounts_oct GROUP BY city -- sort, the highest churn first ;
Exercise 3 · the monthly mart

Compare each city's churn in May and in October

Why. The CFO says churn has not changed since May. That is true for the company. Check it city by city.

The mart w6_churn_month has one row per city and month. CASE WHEN month = '2026-05' THEN cancellations END keeps May's value and leaves the other months empty, so SUM(...) of it is May's count.

Your task

  1. Three columns: city, May's churn in %, October's churn in %, both rounded to 2 decimals.
  2. One row per city. The order does not matter.
SELECT city, ROUND(100.0 * SUM(CASE WHEN month = '2026-05' THEN cancellations END) / SUM(CASE WHEN month = '2026-05' THEN accounts END), 2) AS may_pct -- add October's churn the same way FROM w6_churn_month GROUP BY city;
Exercise 4 · WHERE + GROUP BY

Split Barcelona's October churn by tenure band

Why. Barcelona's churn rose. Either all its subscribers leave more often, or the mix changed: more new subscribers, who always leave more often.

Your task

  1. Keep only Barcelona's accounts.
  2. Four columns: tenure_band, the accounts, the cancellations, the churn in % rounded to 2 decimals. The order does not matter.
SELECT tenure_band -- the accounts, the cancellations, the churn in % FROM w6_accounts_oct -- keep only Barcelona GROUP BY tenure_band;
Exercise 5 · a counterfactual

Compute Barcelona's churn without the 1-euro promotion's accounts

Why. If Barcelona's rise comes from the summer promotion, Barcelona without those accounts should look like May (2.49 %).

Your task

  1. Keep Barcelona's accounts that did NOT join with the promotion (promo_1eur = 0).
  2. One row, three columns: the accounts, the cancellations, the churn in % rounded to 2 decimals.
SELECT COUNT(*) AS accounts -- the cancellations and the churn in % FROM w6_accounts_oct WHERE city = 'Barcelona' -- and keep only the accounts without the promotion ;
Exercise 6 · CASE: a group you can act on

Find the new subscribers who will probably leave: promotion × rides in the first 14 days

Why. A finding is useful when someone can find the accounts and act before they leave. The first 14 days are when a new subscriber builds the habit, or not.

CASE WHEN x <= 2 THEN '0-2' WHEN x <= 5 THEN '3-5' ELSE '6+' END turns a number into a group. You can GROUP BY two columns at once.

Your task

  1. Keep the accounts in their first 3 months (tenure_band = '1-3'), in every city.
  2. Five columns: promo_1eur, the rides group ('0-2', '3-5', '6+' of rides_first_14_days), the accounts, the cancellations, the churn in % rounded to 2 decimals.
  3. Six rows. The order does not matter.
SELECT promo_1eur, CASE WHEN rides_first_14_days <= 2 THEN '0-2' -- finish the groups END AS rides_group -- the accounts, the cancellations, the churn in % FROM w6_accounts_oct WHERE tenure_band = '1-3' GROUP BY promo_1eur, rides_group;
