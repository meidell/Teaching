Step 1. Learn why ÒRBITA needs a data warehouse

The billing system cannot calculate "monthly churn by city", because it has no city column and no rides

Monthly churn is the share of paying accounts on the 1st of the month that cancel during the month. "By city" splits it into ÒRBITA's six cities. Billing fails for three reasons:

  1. The data is in other systems. Billing's columns are subscriber_id, plan, status, status_since and mrr_eur. The city is only in telematics, next to each ride. The tickets are in the support desk, which knows a customer only by email, not by subscriber number.
  2. Billing counts differently. In September billing had 47,700 accounts on its books, because it counts paused, trial and unpaid accounts. Finance counted 40,100 paying subscribers on the same day. The KPI counts paused accounts as paying and trial accounts as not paying, and billing does not know that rule.
  3. The question would slow down the payments. Churn by city for twelve months reads every subscription of the year. Billing runs on the machine that charges customers' cards at that moment.

A database that runs the business, with many small writes, is called operational (OLTP). A database that measures it, with a few large reads, is called analytic (OLAP). The warehouse is the analytic one.

OLTP: online transaction processing. OLAP: online analytical processing. Say the abbreviations once; the page uses them in the sort.
Step 1. Learn why ÒRBITA needs a data warehouse

A warehouse refuses a wrong row when it is loaded, a lake stores the file as it arrived, and a lakehouse checks files on cheap storage

Data warehouseData lakeLakehouse
What it storesIt stores tables whose columns are fixed and documented.It stores files of any kind as they arrived: CSV, JSON, logs, images.It stores files in open table formats (Delta Lake, Apache Iceberg) on cheap cloud storage.
What happens to ÒRBITA's ride with km = "abc"The warehouse refuses the row when it is loaded, because km must be a number. This is called schema on write.The lake stores the telematics file with the wrong row in it. Whoever reads the file later has to notice. This is called schema on read.A control layer checks the file when it is written and refuses it.
Who reads itSQL queries and dashboards read it: the board's numbers.Data scientists and engineers read it, often in Python.Both groups read it, from one copy of the data.
How it usually failsIt is slow and expensive to change when a new question arrives.After a while nobody knows what a file means.It is newer and has more parts that must work together.

A schema is the list of the columns of a table and of what each column holds. Source for the lakehouse: Armbrust, Ghodsi, Xin and Zaharia, CIDR 2021.

Step 3. Learn which columns hold numbers and which describe them

One row of the ride table holds numbers (facts) and keys that point to the tables that describe the ride (dimensions)

Ride 88120 from Session 1, as one row of the warehouse table fact_ride:

ride_iddate_keycity_keysubscriber_keykmduration_minfailed_unlock
8812020261005155214.2250

The key city_key = 1 points to row 1 of the table dim_city:

city_keycity_namecountrytime_zonecurrency
1BarcelonaSpainEurope/MadridEUR
  • A fact (also called a measure) is a number the ride produces, which you add up: km, duration_min, failed_unlock (1 if the ride was a failed unlock).
  • A dimension describes the row, and you filter or group by it: the date, the city, the subscriber. Each dimension has its own table, here dim_city.
  • A key is the number in the central table that points to one row of a dimension table.
The ride started at 22:40 UTC on 4 October. Barcelona is two hours ahead in October, so it started at 00:40 on 5 October local time: date_key is 20261005, not 20261004. That is Session 1's time-zone trap. subscriber_key 5521 is an illustrative warehouse number.

Step 3. Learn which columns hold numbers and which describe them

Three terms describe how the describing tables are built, each with ÒRBITA's example

  • Date dimension. It is a table with one row for each calendar day, with the weekday, the month, the quarter and whether the day is a public holiday. Example: the row for 5 October 2026 says Monday, October, Q4, no holiday. Every central table points to it, so "rides on weekdays only" becomes a filter.
  • Shared (conformed) dimension. It is a describing table that two central tables use. dim_city is used by fact_ride and by fact_spend, so rides and ad spend per city fit in one chart. That works only because "Milano" in telematics and "Milan" in marketing are made into one row.
  • Surrogate key. It is a number the warehouse gives each row of a describing table, instead of reusing a system's own number. The support desk knows Anna Kowalski only by email, and billing by subscriber number. In the warehouse she is one row with one key, for example 5521, and both systems' data joins to it.
Anna Kowalski is the customer from Session 1 whose email changed (anna.k@ and a.kowalski@).
Step 3. Learn which columns hold numbers and which describe them

When Anna changes her email, the warehouse keeps her old row and adds a new one (a type 2 change)

rowsubscriber_keyemailvalid_fromvalid_to
15521anna.k@example.com2026-03-012026-10-08
25521a.kowalski@example.com2026-10-09(open)

Overwriting the old email is called a type 1 change, and it loses the history. A type 2 change closes the old row with an end date and opens a new row, so her September tickets stay linked to her September email. A table whose rows change like this is called a slowly changing dimension.

There are three kinds of central table. A transaction table has one row per event, such as a ride. A periodic snapshot has one row per thing per period, such as an account per month. An accumulating snapshot has one row per thing that moves through stages and is updated at each stage, such as a ticket that is opened, answered and resolved.

The dates are illustrative: Session 1 shows the new email on a ticket of 9 October.

Step 5. Say where every column comes from (part 2 of the note)

The sensitivity cell says whether a column is about a person, and the GDPR decides that

The GDPR is the EU's data-protection law. It calls data that identifies a person personal data. A customer number that can be linked back to a person is still personal data (GDPR Recital 26). ÒRBITA operates in the EU, so every personal column needs an owner and a stated purpose.

ColumnWriteWhy
dim_subscriber.emailPersonalit names a person
fact_ride.subscriber_keyPersonalit can be linked back to the person

dim_city.city_namePublicit could be published as it is
Teams most often forget that the subscriber key is personal data.
