Session 4 · Step 1

ETL means three things happen to the data between the systems that run the business and the warehouse that measures it

ExtractCopy the rows out of each system, unchanged, into a staging area: tables that keep the data exactly as it arrived.
TransformChange the rows to fit the warehouse: one clock, one currency, one spelling, and remove what should not be counted. Every change follows a written rule.
LoadWrite the rows into the warehouse tables. A good load can run twice without creating duplicates: it is idempotent.

Many companies now load first and transform inside the warehouse with SQL. That order is called ELT. The rules are the same; only the place where they run changes.

Ask: in the bank where you have an account, what would "transform" be? Converting every transaction to one currency, removing test transactions. Step 1 ends with a sort of six operations into E, T and L.

Is this ride on 4 October or on 5 October?
5 October. The time is in UTC, the world clock. Barcelona is two hours ahead in October (summer time), so the ride started at 00:40 on 5 October. The row passes all five checks: its date is not empty, the city is on the list, the subscriber exists, the ride is long enough. Only a rule ("convert to the city's local time, then take the date") gets it right.
