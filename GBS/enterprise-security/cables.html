    Why a trader in Geneva depends on these cables

    Arvane's traders in Geneva send orders to the company's Singapore desk all day, and that traffic leaves Europe at the cable landing stations of Marseille, Toulon or Genoa and runs through the Mediterranean, past Egypt and through the Red Sea, so a cut there makes every order slower, or stops it until the telecom provider moves the traffic to another cable. Arvane's terminal in Constanța sends its data to Geneva over land networks or over the few cables in the Black Sea, which come ashore at Mangalia in Romania and at Varna and Balchik in Bulgaria, 40 to 120 km from the terminal.

    Your task: three facts for the decision on Arvane's weakest link

    Why. In Step 6 of Session 5, your team chooses the physical link whose loss would hurt Arvane most. A choice that names the actual cables, and the places where they come ashore, is one the board can check. The three questions below give you those facts.

    Writing your answers here is optional. They are kept in this browser only and are not sent to anyone; copy them into your team's notes.

    1. Name the cables that link southern France and Singapore. Press Show Arvane's routes, then click each gold line in the Mediterranean, or each name in the list beside the map. In the list of landing points (the places where a cable comes ashore), look for a place in France (Marseille or Toulon) and for Singapore. Write the name of every cable that lands in both.
    2. Count the cables that land in Egypt. Choose Egypt in the box "Show only the cables that land in this country". Then move the year slider to 2021, so that the count includes only the cables that were already carrying traffic in November 2021, when the data was published. Write the number, and the names of two places in Egypt where several cables come ashore.
    3. Say what a cut in the Red Sea does to Arvane's Singapore desk. In February and March 2024, three cables in the Red Sea (AAE-1, Seacom and EIG) were damaged, most likely by the anchor of the cargo ship Rubymar, which drifted after a Houthi missile hit it. Seacom said that the repair permit "could take up to eight weeks to obtain". On 6 September 2025, two more cables, SeaMeWe-4 and IMEWE, were cut near Jeddah, and Microsoft reported "increased delays" for its cloud traffic through the Middle East. Find AAE-1, SeaMeWe-4 and IMEWE with the search box and look at where each one comes ashore. Then write two sentences: does the Singapore desk lose its connection, or does it only become slower, and which question should Arvane put to its telecom provider? Sources: Seacom via TechCentral, 8 March 2024; Data Center Knowledge and gCaptain, 7 March 2024 (the Rubymar's anchor as the likely cause); Microsoft via AFP, 7 September 2025. The delay of the longer route round Africa is computed in Session 5, Step 2.

    Where the data comes from, and what it does not show

    The cables, their owners, lengths, dates and landing points come from TeleGeography's Submarine Cable Map, in the version TeleGeography published openly on 12 November 2021, under the licence Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 3.0 (CC BY-NC-SA 3.0). That version is the latest one that may be copied: since 2022, TeleGeography sells its cable data under a paid licence, and its live map at submarinecablemap.com may be looked at but not copied.

    So this map shows the network as it stood in November 2021. It holds 497 cable systems; TeleGeography counted 607 in service in 2026. Cables that were still being built in 2021, such as 2Africa and PEACE, are drawn with a dashed line and their planned year; cables announced after November 2021 are missing. Before you quote a cable's status in a report, check it on submarinecablemap.com.

    The lines are schematic. TeleGeography draws each route by hand to show which places a cable connects; the exact path on the sea floor is not public. On this page the routes are simplified and the coordinates rounded to about 1 km so that the map loads quickly. The simplified files are shared under the same licence: data/cables-2021.json and the licence text. The country shapes are from Natural Earth (1:50 million scale), which is in the public domain. The places marked in gold for Arvane (Geneva, Constanța, Singapore) are real cities; Arvane Commodities SA itself is fictional.
