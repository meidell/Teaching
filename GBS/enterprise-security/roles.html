Who answers for it? The seat above the tripwire

Read before Session 9 (the tabletop) and Session 10 (governance). About ten minutes. Nothing on this page is counted.

Session 2 wrote Arvane's tripwires: the conditions that trigger action without a meeting. A tripwire is only as good as the seat it reports to. In a large company that seat has a name, duties and a budget. This page describes it as it exists in practice, sets out the three lines of defence the role sits inside, and names the body through which such roles talk to each other across borders. Arvane has none of this yet. Session 9 gives it the seat; Session 10 asks who answers when the seat fails.

1 · The incident director

Below is the role at the top of the incident chain in a large technology company, written from the job profile of a practitioner who holds it. The person is not named and nothing is quoted; each line is what the role does, with what it would mean at Arvane's scale underneath. Read it as a job you could hold in ten years, not as a description of someone else.

Incident director · global escalation pointone seat · reports to the CISO · owns the approach
1Decides the organisation's approach to a severe incident. Every major or critical incident, anywhere in the company, ends on this desk. Accountability for the response sits here, not with whoever noticed first.At Arvane: the person who says, at 02:00 on a Saturday, "isolate the terminal network, do not pay, call Bern" — and answers for it on Monday.
2Designs and runs dry-run exercises to test whether the response actually works, and refines the process from what the exercises and the post-incident reviews find.At Arvane: Session 9 is that exercise. The protocol you wish you had had is the review.
3Leads the investigation: technical analysis and triage, a complete written record of the incident, and the CISO kept informed throughout.At Arvane: the log from tripwire part 1 is the start of that record. A lawyer, an insurer or a regulator will read it.
4Provides security expertise on demand to projects while they are being built, not after.At Arvane: the new terminal management system, the vessel tracking app, the bank connection — each gets a review before go-live.
5Oversees the security services: engineering, consulting, design review, code audit, penetration testing, vulnerability management and automated validation — across tens of thousands of software launches and hundreds of vendors a year.At that scale no team reads the code by hand. The defence is a gate every release passes through automatically. Session 5 is about what happens when such a gate is missing, or trusted too much.
6Manages the mitigation of product security issues, whether the product is the company's own or a third party's.At Arvane: the chartering software it buys, the GNSS receivers on twelve ships, the terminal's control system. Arvane wrote none of them and depends on all of them.
7Sets the team structure up to the third line of defence, including which parts are outsourced and on what contractual terms.At Arvane: who is first line, who is second, who audits them — and what the contract with the outsourced SOC in Bucharest says about a Friday night.
8Represents the company in FIRST, the global forum of incident response teams, and gets the most out of its membership.At Arvane: the channel through which a Geneva trader would learn, on the day, that the same malware hit a shipping line in Rotterdam.
Why a seat, not a committeeDuties 1 and 3 are the point of the role: one person decides the approach, and one person owns the record. A committee convened at 02:00 produces neither. Session 2's salami game showed what happens without the seat — the line moved with every slice because nobody owned it.

2 · The three lines of defence

Duty 7 names a model most companies, banks and regulators use to say who answers for what. It is simple, it is on the midterm's reading list in spirit, and Session 10 turns it on Arvane.

First line

Operations own the risk

The people who run the business run its risks. They follow the controls, and they are the first to see something wrong.

At Arvane: the terminal manager in Constanța, the chartering desk, the IT team that applies updates, the captain who sees the GNSS position jump.
Second line

Security, risk and compliance set the rules and watch

They write the policy, run the monitoring, train the first line, and report upward. The incident director sits here.

At Arvane: the CISO, the compliance officer who reads NIS2 and the Swiss reporting duty, the outsourced SOC that watches the logs.
Third line

Internal audit checks both, independently

Reports to the board's audit committee, not to management. Asks whether the first two lines do what they say, and tells the board when they do not.

At Arvane: the auditors who ask, after Session 9's bad weekend, whether the tripwires existed on paper only.
The model is a description of accountability, not of headcount: in a company of Arvane's size one person may hold a first-line and a second-line hat, which is exactly the weakness a regulator or an attacker looks for. The question is never "who has the job title" but "who answers when it fails".

Which line failed?

Six things that could go wrong at Arvane. Pick the line whose failure it is. Local to this device; nothing is recorded.

3 · FIRST

FIRST, the Forum of Incident Response and Security Teams, is the global association of computer security incident response teams, founded in 1990 after the Morris worm showed that no single team could handle an incident that crossed networks and borders. Its members are the response teams of companies, universities, governments and vendors; it publishes the CVSS scoring system used to rate every vulnerability you will hear quoted this term, and the Traffic Light Protocol that says how far a piece of shared intelligence may travel.

For the course the point is duty 8 above: an incident director's value on a bad day depends on who picks up the phone in another company. FIRST is the institution that makes that call possible between a Geneva trader, a Rotterdam shipping line and a software vendor in California — which is the "shared infrastructure" argument the course home page opens with, applied to people rather than cables.

Membership directory and standards: first.org ↗. Look up who in Switzerland is a member; the answer is a useful fact for Session 10.

5 · The Session 9 scorecard

Observers score each team on three questions, 0 to 2 each, after every inject. The questions are duties 1 to 3 turned into checks. They are published now so that nobody can say the rules were invented on the night.

Did the decision on the approach come from the seat that owns it? Not from the loudest person, not from a vote, not from nobody.2 the director decided, and said so · 1 a decision happened, owner unclear · 0 no decision, or several
Was the escalation made at the right slice? Against the team's own tripwires from Session 2.2 on the tripwire · 1 one inject late · 0 after the journalist called
Is there a record a lawyer could read? Who knew what, when, and who was told — including the CISO and the board.2 timestamped and complete · 1 partial · 0 in people's heads
