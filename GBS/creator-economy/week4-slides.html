Three words used all afternoon

Content creator · sponsored post · CPA

Content creator

A person who publishes videos or posts on Instagram, TikTok or YouTube under their own name, has followers there, and earns money from them. One way: a brand pays them to show a product. The older word is influencer.

Sponsored post

A video or post the content creator publishes because a brand paid a fee for it. Today: one video or post plus two stories, with a link and a discount code, live for 30 days. The law requires it to be marked as paid.

CPA · cost per acquisition

What the brand paid for one sale: all the fees divided by all the sales the posts brought. CHF 2,000 of fees and 25 pairs sold = CPA of CHF 80. Lower is better. It is the one number brands use to compare content creators.

Today the product is a pair of headphones at CHF 199 with a margin of CHF 90. A CPA above CHF 90 loses money on every pair.
Ask: "If I pay a creator CHF 500 and the post sells 10 pairs, what is the CPA?" (50). "And if it sells 4?" (125, which is more than the 90 margin: every pair sold loses CHF 35.)

Step 2 · 20 minutes · instructor

A fee becomes a sale in four steps, and each step loses most of what went in

A · Who sees itThe platform decides who is shown the post. Not the follower count.
B · Who taps and buysOf those who see it, a few tap the link; of those, a few buy.
C · How it is countedThe brand can only pay for sales it can trace to the post.
D · What the fee buysA flat fee per post, priced by followers, not by sales.

Why this matters at 15:04. In Step 4 the teams take turns choosing content creators from a pool, and most teams choose the one with the most followers. The follower count appears in none of the four steps. The team that wins reads a profile for A, B, C and D instead.

Each step has a small calculator on the page. We go through them in order; type along.
Keep the flow on the screen for 30 seconds. Then one slide per letter.
A · Who sees the post

A follower is someone who once pressed Follow. Whether they see the next post is decided again every time.

Instagram is four places, each with its own ranking (the program that decides which posts you see and in which order; Mosseri, Instagram's head, 31 May 2023). Feed, the home screen: mostly accounts you follow, ordered by what you have liked and watched, by the post's own numbers, and by whether you usually interact with that account. So a follower who ignores an account stops seeing it. Reels and Explore: mostly accounts you do not follow, chosen by how the post performs with its first viewers.

TikTok says it outright: "neither follower count nor whether the account has had previous high-performing videos are direct factors" (TikTok Newsroom, 18 June 2020). A video is shown to a first group, and spreads only if they watch it to the end.

So a sponsored post reaches two groups: the followers who interact with the account regularly, often well under half of them, plus whoever Reels, Explore or For You add if the post performs. The follower count predicts neither.
Ask the room: "How many of the accounts you follow did you actually see today?" Most say a handful of the hundreds they follow. That is the point.
A · The number to read instead

Read the median views of the last ten posts, not the followers and not the average

Median, not mean

Sort the views of the last ten posts and take the middle value. One viral video pulls the average up and will not happen again on command; it does not move the median. On the page, @techbro.ch: 410,000 followers, one video at 1.2 million views, median 22,000. The 22,000 is what a brand gets.

Engagement rate: always ask "divided by what"

Likes and comments divided by followers, or by views. HypeAuditor's 2025 report gives Instagram 1.59 % (by followers, 2024) and 10.3 % for TikTok accounts under 10,000 followers. The two are not comparable: on TikTok a small account's video is watched by far more people than follow it.

On the page, Step 2 A, "Try it": two profiles, X with 27 times the followers of Y. Before you look at the table, decide which one more people will actually see. Then compare the three ratios.
Two minutes on the toy. X has 27× the followers, Y has the higher median views. Most of the room guesses X.
B · Who taps and buys

Sales are a multiplication of four small shares, so halving any one of them doubles the cost per sale

sales = views × action rate × share who can buy × shop conversion rate   ·   CPA = fee ÷ sales
ShareWhat it isWhere it comes from
Action rateof the people who see the post, the share who tap the link or note the codedepends on whether the audience wants this kind of product at all; a guess before the campaign
Share who can buythe share of the audience who live where the shop ships; today, Switzerland onlythe content creator's audience insights (country), a screenshot the brand asks for
Shop conversion rateof the visitors who reach the shop, the share who buythe brand's own analytics: 3.0 % for Sonnaz Audio; the creator cannot change it

Worked example. 20,000 views × 1 % tap × 80 % in Switzerland × 3 % buy = 4.8 sales. Fee CHF 400 → CPA = 400 ÷ 4.8 = CHF 83, just under the CHF 90 margin. Now a big French channel: 140,000 views × 1 % × 6 % in Switzerland × 3 % = 2.5 sales. Fee CHF 5,500 → CPA CHF 2,200.

Seven times the views, a quarter of the sales, 26 times the CPA, because 94 % of the audience cannot buy. Point at "share who can buy": it is the gate that kills big foreign channels for a Swiss-only shop.
B · Try it

The funnel calculator on the page: find the views a fee needs to break even

Step 2 B, the calculator. Type: fee 400, views 20,000, action rate 1, share who can buy 80, shop conversion 3. Read the sales (4.8) and the CPA (83). Now set the share who can buy to 6, a large French channel. Read the last box: how many views would the same fee now need to break even at CHF 90?

Answer: 400 ÷ 90 = 4.4 sales needed; 4.4 ÷ (0.01 × 0.06 × 0.03) = about 247,000 views, twelve times more. That is the arithmetic behind "a Swiss brand should not pay a French channel".

Write one sentence in the box under the calculator: which of the four shares will hurt your CPA most for a Swiss headphone brand?
Press → to reveal the answer after a minute. Most teams find 240,000–250,000.
C · How the sale is counted

A CPA is only as good as the count of sales under it, and all three ways of counting miss something

A tagged link

The brand gives each creator a link with tags after the "?": utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign, utm_content (Google Analytics help). A visit on that link that ends in a purchase is credited to that creator. Misses: the viewer who watches, then types the shop address a week later.

A discount code

Counts buyers who never clicked: they typed the code. Misses in the other direction: codes leak to coupon websites that rank in Google for "brand discount code", and buyers who never saw the creator use them.

An affiliate link

A cookie on the buyer's browser credits the creator if they buy within a window, often weeks. Broken by cleared cookies, a second device, or buying in a shop.

Last click. Most shop analytics give the whole sale to the last click before buying. Watched a review on Monday, searched the brand on Friday: credited to search, not to the creator. So a measured CPA usually overstates the true one for creators who build interest, and understates it when codes leak. The clean fix is a holdout, a comparable group that does not see the campaign: Session 6.

Step 2 C, "Try it": build one creator's tagged link. Change utm_content for their second story and see what the brand can now tell apart.
Two minutes. The point for the draft: a creator whose audience buys later, from a laptop, looks worse in the data than they are.
D · What the fee buys

The fee follows the followers; the sales follow views × audience fit × share who can buy. A team that chooses by views and audience gets more sales for less money.

Content creators quote a flat fee per deliverable, here one video or post plus two stories, not a price per sale. The fee rises with the follower count, because that is what the market can see, and with the extras: usage rights (the brand may reuse the video in its own ads for a period), exclusivity (no competing brand for a period), and running the post as a paid ad from the creator's account.

In a survey of more than 600 marketing professionals, about half said they pay nano and micro content creators (under 100,000 followers) under USD 500 per piece of content; mid-tier creators were spread across USD 2,000 to 10,000 (Influencer Marketing Hub, Benchmark Report).

A creator with 120,000 followers whose posts reach 6,500 people costs CHF 2,400. A creator with 15,000 followers whose videos reach 18,000 people costs CHF 350. Both are on today's cards.
Today the fees on the cards are final. Negotiating them is Session 5.

Step 4 · 18 minutes · the whole class

The teams take turns choosing content creators from the same sixteen, and a content creator one team takes is gone for the others

This way of choosing is called a draft. You may not get your first choice, because another team may take it before your turn.

  1. There are three rounds. In each round every team has one turn, so each team chooses at most three content creators.
  2. The page draws the order of round 1 from the team names. Nobody chooses it. The board on the page shows whose turn it is.
  3. In round 2 the order turns around: the team that chose last chooses first. Round 3 has the same order as round 1.
  4. On your turn, click one content creator on the board, or click "Pass". You cannot choose one whose fee is more than the money your team has left.
  5. Your team has 2 minutes per turn. After 2 minutes the next team may choose, and your team loses its choice for that round.
If the board does not load, the instructor calls out the turns aloud and each team says its choice.
Board on the screen. Call the order aloud. Enforce the two minutes. 15:22 at the latest: move on.

Do this: 1 · For each of your creators, run the funnel calculator in Step 2 B with the card's median views and Swiss share, the shop's 3 %, and your own guess of the action rate. 2 · Add up the sales. 3 · Divide your total fees by that number. 4 · Type the CPA, the sales, and one sentence: which creator carries the list, and which of the four shares is the risk. 5 · Press "Lock our prediction".

Step 6 · the results

The simulation runs each creator's 30 days with values you could not see on the card

For every creator the simulation uses: the views, drawn around the median on the card with some chance; a hidden action rate and audience fit for over-ear headphones; the share in Switzerland from the card; and the shop's 3 %. What the table will show: the cheapest sales come from the small, Swiss, product-relevant accounts (commuters, headphone reviewers); the most expensive from the large accounts whose audience is abroad or is not shopping for headphones. The CPA in the pool runs from about CHF 20 to about CHF 2,000.

Step 6 · what to take from it

The follower count was on every card and predicted nothing. Three numbers did.

Median views

How many people actually see a post. On TikTok it can be above the follower count; on Instagram it is usually far below.

Share in Switzerland

The share of viewers who can buy at all. A 6 % share divides the sales by sixteen compared with a 94 % share.

Audience fit

Whether the niche is one where people buy headphones: commuters, audio reviewers, travellers yes; comedy, gym, gaming no. Not a number on the card, but readable from the niche.

A team that paid for followers paid two or three thousand francs for a handful of sales. A team that paid for views, fit and Swiss audience bought sales at CHF 20 to 80, under the CHF 90 margin.
Ask the team with the lowest CPA how they chose. Then: "Step 7, ten minutes."
