Reverse-engineer a winner

A laptop with Chrome, Edge or Firefox. One per team is enough.

Last week the class asked three AI engines the same headphone question fifteen ways. One review site, rtings.com, was quoted in nine of fourteen answers. The brands that make the headphones were almost never quoted. Today's question is simple: why that site? Six quick checks, each something you can do in a browser in a few minutes, give the answer. Then each team writes three reasons — and tries to break them by finding a page that has the same things and is not quoted.

✅ Step 2 · 20 min

Six checks: what makes a page quotable

An AI answer is built in a chain: a robot fetches pages → the text is filed → pages are ranked → the best paragraphs are picked out → the answer is written from them, with links to its sources. A page can fall out at any link in the chain. Each check below looks at one link, asks one plain question, and has something to click. Every example is real and was looked at on 22 October 2026.

1Can the robots get in?
Every engine sends a robot to copy pages. Before it copies anything, it reads a small text file on the site, robots.txt, which says who may enter. A site can let Google in and keep OpenAI out, or the reverse. If the robot is kept out, the page can never be quoted — however good it is.
Do this: type the site's address followed by /robots.txt — for example rtings.com/robots.txt — and read it. Lines starting User-agent name a robot; Disallow lines say where that robot may not go; * means "everyone else". The robots that matter for AI answers are OAI-SearchBot (ChatGPT's search), PerplexityBot and Googlebot.
Try it · two real files
Pick a robot and press the button.

      

      
What the real files sayrtings.com lets every robot read every review page (it only closes its admin and user-review areas). tomsguide.com names fifteen AI robots — ByteDance, Meta, Apple, Amazon, Mistral, Cohere and others — and shuts them out of the whole site, but says nothing about OpenAI, Perplexity or Google, so those three walk in. reddit.com did not even show its file to an unknown robot: it answered "your request has been blocked due to a network policy". Reddit lets in the companies it has signed deals with (Google and OpenAI, 2024) and nobody else. Being quoted does not mean being open to everyone.

      
Write down: open to all · some AI robots blocked · the quoting engine blocked · could not check.

    


    
    

      
2Is the text actually on the page?

      
What you see in a browser is not always what the robot receives. Many modern sites send an almost empty page and then build the content with JavaScript, a small program that runs in your browser. Google waits and runs that program (slowly, and not for every page); some other engines do not. A page whose text only exists after the program runs may be invisible to them.

      
Do this: on the page, press Ctrl+U (Mac: ⌘⌥U) to see the raw page the server sent, and search it (Ctrl+F) for a sentence you can see on screen. If the sentence is not there, the text arrives by JavaScript. Second way: in Chrome press F12, then Ctrl+Shift+P, type Disable JavaScript, press Enter and reload the page — what remains is what a simple robot gets.

3Do the paragraphs stand on their own?

      
An AI engine does not read whole pages. It cuts every page into short pieces, a paragraph or two each, and picks the few pieces that best fit the question. A piece is quoted on its own, without the rest of the page. "It also lasts 40 hours" is useless on its own — it who? "The Soundcore Q45 lasts 40 hours with noise cancelling on, 10 more than the Sony" can be quoted straight away.

      
Do this: copy a page's text, paste it into the box below, and see how many pieces pass three tests: it names what it is about, it contains a number or a comparison, and it uses the words of the question. Three sample pages are one click away.

4Does the page say what it is, in machine language?

      
A page can carry a hidden label, in a standard format called schema.org, that tells machines "this is a Product, price 199, rating 4.6 out of 5, updated 3 September". Engines read those labels directly and do not have to guess. A page without labels is not punished; a page with them hands over its facts ready-made.

      
Do this: in the raw page (Ctrl+U), search for ld+json. Each hit is a label block; the word after "@type" says what it describes. Or paste the page's address into Google's free Rich Results Test, which lists the labels in plain words.

5Who links to it, and who talks about it?

      
Search engines treat a link from another site as a vote, and a vote from a site that itself has many votes counts more. That is Google's original idea, PageRank, and it is still part of how pages are ranked. The same logic applies to being mentioned by name. A new page on a much-linked site starts with a head start.

      
Do this: paste the page's address into a free backlink checker (Ahrefs or Moz, links at the bottom) and note the number of referring domains — how many different sites link to it. The two tools give different numbers, so note which one you used. Then search Google for the site's name with -site: its own domain, which shows who mentions it without linking.

      

        
Try it · how votes add up

        
Five imaginary sites. Tick who links to whom and watch the scores, computed with PageRank's own formula. Three things to try: tick Retailer → Review site — the review site jumps from 7 to 21, because the retailer is itself well voted. Untick it and tick News site → Review site instead — it barely moves, because nobody votes for the news site. Then tick Forum → Brand page and Forum → Retailer — the forum's one vote is now split three ways, and the review site drops.

6How fresh is it?

      
For questions like "best … 2026", engines prefer pages that were changed recently. Some sites simply change the year in the title without changing the text. The Wayback Machine keeps old copies of most pages, so you can see what really changed and when.

      
Do this: look for an "updated" line on the page; then open the page in the Wayback Machine and compare an old copy with today's. The buttons below build the right links for any address.

Words to keep

    

      
robots.txt · the file on every site that says which robots may copy which pages.

      
User-agent · a robot's name in that file; * means everyone not named.

      
OAI-SearchBot · GPTBot · OpenAI's robot for search answers, and its robot for training. A site can allow one and block the other.

      
Network block · refusing a robot by where it comes from, not by its name (Reddit).

      
Before JavaScript · what the server sends; what a simple robot sees. Ctrl+U shows it.

      
Rendering · running the page's program to build its content. Google does it, late; others may not.

      
Piece (chunk) · the paragraph-sized unit an AI engine picks and quotes, on its own.

      
Stands alone · a piece that names its subject, states a number or comparison, and uses the question's words.

      
Labels (schema.org, JSON-LD) · machine-readable facts hidden in the page: Product, Review, price, rating, date.

      
Referring domains · how many different sites link to a page. The vote count.

      
PageRank · a vote from a well-voted site counts more; a vote from a site that votes for everyone counts less.

      
Wayback Machine · old copies of pages; shows what really changed.

      
Control page · a page with the same properties and a different result. The cheapest way to test a reason.

    


    

      
The open claim · verdict for today

      
"A page can get itself quoted just by rewriting its text — short self-contained paragraphs, concrete comparisons, numbers — without anyone new linking to it."

      
Today added a fact: the winner is, before JavaScript, an almost empty page with one good sentence and no labels. If it is quoted for its text, the engines are reading the rendered version. Session 3's rewrite can put the same kind of sentences in the raw page, in stand-alone paragraphs, with labels, on a page whose links do not change. That is the experiment.
