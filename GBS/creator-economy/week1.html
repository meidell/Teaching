Commit, before you look

Somebody in Geneva is about to spend CHF 200 on noise-cancelling headphones. They will not visit five manufacturer websites. They will ask something — a phone, a search box, a chatbot — and mostly do what it says.

Before you touch a device: which three brands do you think an AI answer engine will name, and which single source do you think it will lean on most? Guess. You are not expected to know, and a wrong prediction written down is worth more to you today than a right one kept vague.

In order, most confident first. Brand names, not model numbers.
A website, a kind of website, or a person. One line. "I have no idea" is an acceptable answer if you write why you have no idea.
Once sealed it stops being editable — that is the point of a prediction.
Why this is not a gimmickThe gap between what you expected and what the machine did is the only thing in this session that is genuinely yours. A fact you are told is worth very little; a fact that contradicts something you committed to ten minutes earlier is very hard to forget. This is also how your participation mark gets evidenced rather than guessed at — every sealed prediction is logged with a timestamp.

The query round

One question, asked fifteen ways. Three engines, five phrasings — and you have one cell of that grid, so between us we cover all fifteen. If everyone asked ChatGPT the same sentence we would learn one thing; this way we learn whether the engine matters, whether the phrasing matters, or neither.

Do this now

1. Ask your engine your phrasing, exactly as written — copy and paste it. 2. Read the answer properly. 3. Write down every source it cites or links, and the brands it names. If it cites nothing, that is a finding: write "no sources". 4. Do not ask a follow-up, and do not clean up the answer to make it look sensible.

One per line. Domains are enough — reddit.com, rtings.com, sony.ch. Everyone's answers are pooled, so the tidier these are the better the wall reads.
Optional, one line. Often the most useful column on the wall.

✅ 14:47 · 16 min · Step 4

Why a machine quotes a stranger

This is the longest I will talk all term, so it had better be the part you cannot work out for yourselves. Everything above you just discovered. Here is the mechanism underneath it.

An answer engine does two separate things, and you can lose at either

It does not "know" about headphones. When you ask, it retrieves — finds documents that look like they answer this question — and then it summarises, lifting the parts it can state confidently. Those are two different competitions. A page can be retrieved and never quoted, because nothing in it survives being pulled out of context. A page can be beautifully quotable and never retrieved, because nothing points at it.

Why a forum thread keeps winning

  • It is phrased like the question. Somebody wrote "best ANC headphones under 200?" in the user's own words. A manufacturer page says "Industry-leading noise cancellation" — which is not a question anybody asks.
  • It contains disagreement. Fifteen people arguing produces the comparative claims a summary needs. One company's page cannot tell you its competitor is better at anything.
  • It is visibly disinterested. The models weight sources partly on how the rest of the web treats them, and a forum where people complain about products they bought reads as somewhere opinions are real.
  • It is shaped for extraction. Question at the top, answers underneath, each one self-contained. That is almost exactly the shape a retrieval system wants.

Why the brand's own page loses

Not because it is bad. Because it is answering a different question — usually "who are we" — in language written to persuade rather than to inform, with an obvious interest in the outcome, and with nothing outside itself vouching for it. Google's own Search Quality Rater Guidelines gave this its vocabulary: E-E-A-T — Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness. Three of those four are judgments the rest of the web makes about you, not claims you make about yourself.

So there are exactly two jobs

Be extractable — state the claim, near the question, in a form that survives being lifted out. Be vouched for — have other people say it about you somewhere the machine already trusts. Most of the money in this industry goes on a third thing, advertising, which buys neither.

And here is what today cannot tell usWe have measured what gets cited. We have not shown that anything can be changed. Maybe structure is enough and a well-built page walks in. Maybe it is hopeless without years of other people linking to you. Nobody in this room knows — so in Session 3 we publish a real page and find out, and we read the result in January.

Two or three sentences, about your result, not the general theory. This is the one I read before next week.

Your team, and your role

Five teams of three, for the whole term. Three is deliberate: in a pair the work splits in half, in a five somebody hides, in a three everybody is visibly load-bearing.

Each session you take one of three roles, and they rotate:

  • Operator — drives the tools. Hands on the keyboard, and responsible for the team actually producing something rather than discussing producing something.
  • Skeptic — must argue against your own team's conclusion before it leaves the table. Not optional, not personal. This role exists so that disagreeing is a job rather than a social risk.
  • Scribe — writes the artifact. Whatever the Scribe does not write down did not happen.
✅ 15:16 · 22 min · Step 6

Three candidate reasons

Take the wall. Your team's job is to explain it — why those sources and not others? Write three candidate reasons. Not three observations: three mechanisms, each one a sentence that could turn out to be false.

The difference, because it is the whole exerciseObservation: "Reddit came up a lot." That cannot be wrong, so it is worth nothing. Mechanism: "It is cited because the thread's title is phrased as the user's question." That predicts something — it says a forum thread with a vague title should lose — and it can be checked. Next week you will try to break each other's.
The most testable one is the most valuable one. It is also the one we will attack first.
✅ 15:38 · 14 min · Step 7

The gallery walk

Off the screens

Scribes: your three reasons go on a sheet on the wall, big enough to read from two metres. Everyone: you get three green stickers and one red dot. Green on the reasons you believe. The red dot goes on the one you think is wrong — and you must be able to say why in one sentence. You may not sticker your own team's sheet.

Then we only discuss the sheets carrying red dots. Five teams presenting in turn would mean four bored teams and about fifty minutes; this takes fourteen and everybody is on their feet.

Name the team and the reason, then one sentence. Attacking well is marked here — this is participation, not rudeness.

I believe that is false, and I am stating it anyway so you have something specific to attack. Bring a real counterexample — a page, a query, a screenshot — and it counts toward your participation. Bring evidence that it is true and it counts double, because that would be the more interesting result and it would change what we do in Session 3.
