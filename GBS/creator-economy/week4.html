✅ Step 2 · instructor · 25 min

How a sponsored post turns into a sale

A brand pays a creator a fee. Between the fee and a sale there are four mechanisms, and each one loses most of what went in. The draft in Step 4 is won by the team that reads a creator's profile for these four mechanisms instead of for the follower count.

AWho actually sees the post — the distribution system decides, not the follower count

A follower count is the number of people who once pressed Follow. Whether they see the next post is decided by a ranking system, every time, for every viewer. The platforms describe these systems themselves:

  • Instagram runs a separate ranking for each part of the app (Mosseri, 31 May 2023). Feed ranks posts mostly from accounts you follow, by your activity, information about the post, information about the person who posted, and your history of interacting with them. Reels and Explore rank posts mostly from accounts you do not follow, with information about the post weighed first. So a post reaches the followers who interact with that account, plus whatever Reels and Explore add.
  • TikTok's For You feed ranks by your interactions (likes, shares, follows, comments, what you watch to the end), information about the video (captions, sounds, hashtags) and, with lower weight, device and account settings. TikTok states that "neither follower count nor whether the account has had previous high-performing videos are direct factors" (TikTok Newsroom, 18 June 2020).
  • YouTube viewers mostly come from search and recommendations around a topic. A review keeps getting views for months, while a TikTok's views mostly arrive in the first days.

The consequence: the number that predicts how many people will see a sponsored post is views on the creator's recent posts, not followers. Use the median of the last 10 posts (the middle value), not the mean (the average). One viral video drags the mean up and will not happen again on command.

Engagement rate (ER) is likes and comments divided by something, and the something changes the answer. HypeAuditor reports Instagram's average ER at 1.59 % in 2024, down from 1.85 % in 2023, and an average of 10.3 % for TikTok creators with fewer than 10,000 followers (HypeAuditor, 2025). The two are not comparable: on TikTok a small account's video can be watched by many more people than follow it, so ER divided by followers comes out high. Always ask which denominator a benchmark used.

BFrom a view to a sale — the funnel, and why it is a multiplication

Every viewer has to pass four gates. Each gate keeps a small share, and the shares multiply:

sales = views × action rate × share who can buy × shop conversion rate CPA = fee ÷ sales
  • Action rate — the share of viewers who tap the link (a link sticker in a story, a link in the description, a link in bio) or note the discount code. It depends on whether the audience is looking for this kind of product at all.
  • Share who can buy — a Swiss brand that ships only to Switzerland cannot sell to the 94 % of a French creator's audience who live in France. The creator's own audience insights (country, city, age) give this share; ask for a screenshot.
  • Shop conversion rate — the share of visitors to the shop who buy. The brand knows its own number from its analytics. It is not the creator's to change.

Because the gates multiply, halving any one of them doubles the CPA. A creator with twice the views but half the share who can buy brings the same number of sales.

Set the share who can buy to 6 % — a large French channel. How many views would the same fee then need to break even? The calculator's last box answers it.
CHow the sale is counted — links, codes, and the sales nobody sees

A CPA is only as good as the count of sales under it. There are three ways to count, and each misses something:

  • A tagged link. The brand gives each creator a link with UTM parameters — tags added after a ? that analytics tools read. Google Analytics documents utm_source (the referrer), utm_medium (the marketing medium), utm_campaign (the product or promotion) and utm_content ("used to differentiate creatives"). A visit that arrives on that link, and a purchase in the same visit, is credited to that creator.
  • A discount code. Counts buyers who never clicked: they watched, then typed the shop address. But codes leak: they get copied to coupon websites, which rank in Google for "brand discount code" — the same search mechanics as Sessions 1–3 — and then buyers who never saw the creator use them.
  • An affiliate link. Sets a cookie on the buyer's browser that credits the creator if the buyer purchases within a window the programme sets (often weeks). Cleared cookies, a second device, or buying in a shop break the trail.

Most shop analytics give the whole sale to the last click before the purchase. A viewer who watched a review on Monday and searched the brand on Friday is credited to search, not to the creator. So a measured CPA usually overstates the true one for creators who build interest, and understates it when codes leak. The only clean fix is a holdout: a comparable region or group that does not see the campaign. Session 6 comes back to this.

Try it · build one creator's tagged link
Change utm_content for the creator's second story. The brand can now see which of the two stories sold — and nothing about the viewer who bought a week later from a laptop.
DWhat the fee buys — and why it is not priced per sale

Creators quote a flat fee per deliverable (here: one video or post plus two stories), not per sale. The fee rises with the follower count, because that is what the market can see, and with the extras attached: usage rights (the brand may reuse the video in its own ads, for a period), exclusivity (no competing brand for a period), and running the post as a paid ad from the creator's account. In a survey of more than 600 marketing professionals, about half reported paying nano and micro creators under USD 500 per piece of content, while mid-tier creators were spread across USD 2,000–10,000 (Influencer Marketing Hub, Influencer Marketing Benchmark Report).

So the fee follows followers, while sales follow views × audience fit × share who can buy. The gap between the two is where a team wins or loses the draft.

The brief and the pool

The brief · Sonnaz Audio SA · fictional company, real mechanics Sonnaz Audio launches the Sonnaz Q1, over-ear noise-cancelling headphones, at CHF 199 on 20 November — the same buying question your class measured in Session 1, now from the brand's side.
· Sold only in its own online shop, which ships only within Switzerland.
· Gross margin per pair: CHF 90. A sale that costs more than CHF 90 loses money. Target: CHF 60.
· The brand's own data: visitors who arrive from social media links buy at 3.0 %.
· Each team's budget: CHF 6,000, for at most 3 creators. Deliverable for every creator: one video or post plus two stories, with a tagged link and a code, live for 30 days.
· The fees on the cards are final: no negotiating today. That is Session 5.

✅ Step 4 · 20 min · the whole class

The draft

Three rounds. The order reverses each round (1→last, last→1, 1→last), so the team that picks first in round 1 picks last in round 2. The order of round 1 is drawn from the team names, so nobody chooses it. When it is your turn, pick one creator or pass. A creator another team has picked is gone. You cannot pick a creator your remaining budget does not cover.

If the team whose turn it is has not picked after 2 minutes, the next team may pick, and the slow team loses that round's pick.

Terms from today
CPA — cost per acquisition: fees ÷ sales.
Break-even CPA — the margin on one sale; above it, each sale loses money.
Median views — the middle value of the last 10 posts; one viral post does not move it.
Engagement rate — likes and comments ÷ followers, or ÷ views. Always ask which.
Follower graph / interest graph — posts shown because you follow the account, or because the system predicts you will watch.
Action rate — the share of viewers who tap the link or note the code.
UTM parameters — tags on a link (utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign, utm_content) that analytics tools read.
Last-click attribution — the whole sale goes to the last click before buying.
Holdout — a comparable group kept out of the campaign, to measure what it really added.
Deliverable · usage rights · exclusivity — what a fee buys.
