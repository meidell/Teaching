Step 1 · one person per team

The weekly check: today's 9 rows go into your team's measurement sheet

  1. Open the team's Google Sheet. Add today's date and the next week number. If your team skipped a week, count the weeks since your page went online.
  2. In Google Search Console, paste your page's address in the top bar: write whether it says "URL is on Google" and the date of the last crawl. Do the same in Bing Webmaster Tools.
  3. Ask your 3 test questions word for word in ChatGPT (Search on), Perplexity and Google: 9 rows. For each, write whether your page is quoted and which websites are.
Everyone else starts reading Step 2 now. A row that nobody wrote down today cannot be filled in later from memory: write "not recorded".
One person per team. Check two sheets as you walk around. Teams without a page online: they republish during Step 2 and write today as Week 0.

Step 4 · team work · the main task of today

Find the content creator who bought followers: five cards, one fake, the report does not say which

  1. Open each of the five content creators' cards in Step 4: the followers and median views over 12 weeks, reach, likes, comments, countries, clicks and sales.
  2. For each card, tick the signs you see. One sign proves nothing; several at once, with no innocent explanation that fits them all, do.
  3. Choose your verdict. Write the two strongest pieces of evidence, with the numbers (at most 120 characters).
  4. Name one content creator who shows a sign but did not buy followers, and the innocent explanation.
  5. One person sends the team's verdict. The answer and the class's verdicts open.
Visit each team and each online room. If a team is stuck, ask: "Which account gained followers while its views did not move?" Do not say the name.
Step 5 · team work

Rewrite the report so that a finance director would accept it

A finance director accepts a number only if it was counted, not estimated, its counting rule is written down, and it is hard to buy.

  1. For each of the 11 numbers, choose: keep in the report, keep as context only, or drop. The table below them shows the report your choices produce.
  2. Write your team's counting rule for sales: the number of days after the post that count, the discount code, double counting, returns.
  3. One person sends the team's standard to the board.
If the page warns "you keep …", the team kept a number that can be bought: ask them to justify it in the counting rule or drop it.
Step 6 · big screen · the whole class

The class's measurement standard: the numbers every report in your playbook must show

A number becomes part of the class standard when more than half of the teams keep it.

Each team writes and signs one sentence: "In our playbook, a campaign is judged by …". Your January playbook is checked against this standard.
Show Step 6 on your own screen: refresh the tally, read the model paragraph, ask two teams to read their sentence aloud.
