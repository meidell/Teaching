✅ Intro · instructor · 20 min

How a new page gets quoted

Three things must happen before an AI tool quotes a brand-new page. A fourth rule lets you learn from the experiment. Every step today matches one of these letters.

AThe AI tool's robot must find the page.

AI tools send out automatic programs, called robots or crawlers, that visit websites and read them. If no robot visits your page, no AI can quote it. Pages usually get missed because the site blocks robots, because no other page links to them, or because they sit behind a login.

Today: Steps 4 and 5.
BThe page must answer a question people actually ask.

An AI only quotes a page when someone asks a question the page answers. Write for one exact question, such as "How much do brands pay a TikTok creator with 5,000 followers?", not a broad topic like "creator income".

Today: Step 1.
COne of its paragraphs must be picked over other websites' paragraphs.

The AI reads several pages that answer the same question and quotes only a few paragraphs. It tends to pick a paragraph that gives the answer in its first sentence, makes sense on its own, and has specific facts such as numbers, names and dates.

Today: Step 3.
DTo learn what worked, change one thing at a time.

Say you rewrite a paragraph, and the same week you change the title, add links and share the page on Instagram. If the page then gets quoted, you cannot tell which of the four changes did it. So during the experiment you change only the words you are testing, keep everything else the same, and measure the same way every time. Then any change in the results was caused by the words.

Today: Steps 2 and 6.
✅ Step 1 · 10 min · rule B

Pick one question

Your team chooses one question that a real person would type into ChatGPT, and that your page will answer.

Make up a short team name. It goes on your page, your sheet and the class list.
  1. Each team member writes down 3 questions about the creator economy that a creator, a brand or a student might ask. Write them exactly as someone would type them, as a full question.
    Examples of the kind of question meant:
    A creator might ask: "How many followers do I need on TikTok to join the Creator Rewards Program?" · "Do I have to pay tax in Switzerland on gifted products from brands?" · "How much should I charge for a YouTube video mention with 20,000 subscribers?"
    A brand might ask: "How much does a micro-influencer campaign cost in Switzerland?" · "Do influencers in Switzerland have to mark a post as an ad?" · "What engagement rate is good for an Instagram account with 50,000 followers?"
    A student might ask: "Can I earn money on Instagram as a student in Switzerland without a business?" · "How long does it take to get 1,000 followers on TikTok?" · "Which platform pays creators more per 1,000 views, YouTube or TikTok?"
  2. Put all the questions on one list and delete any that are too broad. A question is too broad if a whole book could answer it.
    Too broad: "How do creators make money?"
    Good: "How much do Swiss brands pay an Instagram creator with 10,000 followers for one post?"
  3. From what is left, pick the question where you think few good answers exist online yet. Narrow questions about one country, one platform or one follower size work best. With less competition, your page has a better chance of being picked (rule C).
  4. Check that your team can answer it with real facts you can find and link to, such as reports, platform help pages or published price surveys. Don't pick a question you can only answer with guesses.
  5. Write 2 extra versions of the same question, worded the way other people might ask it. For example: "What does a 10k Instagram influencer charge in Switzerland?" You now have 3 test questions.
  6. Type the 3 test questions below. In Step 2 you also write them at the top of your measurement sheet. From now on, never change their wording. If you ask differently next week, you can't compare the results.

✅ Step 3 · 25 min · rule C

Build the page with an AI assistant

You collect real facts, ask an AI assistant to turn them into a web page, save that page as a file on your laptop, and check it. The page must contain the paragraph an AI tool will want to quote.

3a · Collect your facts · 10 min
  1. Search the web for 4–6 facts that answer your question, such as prices, percentages, survey results and platform rules.
  2. For each fact, write in the box below: the fact in one sentence, the name of the source and its web address. One fact per line.
    The format, with an invented example — do not use it: "Creators with 10k–50k followers charge on average CHF 250–500 per Instagram post – [source name], [web address]"
  3. Only keep facts from a page you actually opened and read. Don't copy numbers from a search results snippet or from an AI answer.
3b · Ask the assistant to build the page · 5 min
  1. Go to claude.ai (or ChatGPT) and log in. Click New chat.
  2. Below is the prompt. This page has already filled in your question, your facts, your team name and today's date from what you typed above. Anything still highlighted in [square brackets] is missing: fill it in above, or replace it in the chat before you send.
  3. Click Copy the prompt, paste it into the assistant's text box and press Enter.
3c · Save the page as a file on your laptop · 5 min

First create a folder on your Desktop called ai-page. The file goes in this folder, because Netlify takes a whole folder in Step 4.

Option 1: download the file. If the assistant shows the page as a file or a preview with a Download button (an arrow pointing down), click it. Find the file in your Downloads folder. Rename it to exactly index.html and move it into the ai-page folder.

Option 2: copy and paste the code. Use this if there is no Download button.

  1. Click the Copy button on the code the assistant wrote. The code starts with <!DOCTYPE html> and ends with </html>.
  2. Open a plain text editor:
    • Mac: open TextEdit. In the menu, click Format → Make Plain Text before you paste. Without this step the file won't work.
    • Windows: open Notepad.
  3. Paste the code (Cmd+V on Mac, Ctrl+V on Windows).
  4. Save the file:
    • Mac: File → Save. Name it index.html, choose the ai-page folder, and press Save. If a box asks whether to use .html or .txt, click Use .html.
    • Windows: File → Save As. Choose the ai-page folder. Set Save as type to All files (*.*) and Encoding to UTF-8. Name it index.html and press Save.
3d · Open and check the page · 5 min
  1. Open the ai-page folder and double-click index.html. It opens in your web browser. The address bar shows something starting with file://, which means it is only on your laptop so far.
  2. If you see code instead of a page, the file was saved wrongly. Go back to 3c.
  3. Check the page against this list. Fix anything wrong by asking the assistant to change it, and save again. Tick each box when it is true.
Version AThis is version A of your page. Make a copy of the ai-page folder, call the copy ai-page-version-A, and don't touch that copy again. It is your record of what you published.
✅ Step 4 · 15 min · rule A

Put the page online with Netlify

Netlify is a free service that puts your folder on the internet and gives it a public address. Once it's online, robots can visit it.

4a · Create a free account · 5 min · one team member

Use an account the team can keep using for the whole course.

  1. Go to app.netlify.com/signup.
  2. Choose Sign up with Email (or GitHub or Google if you prefer). Enter an email address and a password.
  3. Open your email inbox, find the message from Netlify and click the confirmation link.
  4. Netlify may ask a few questions about you and your team. Any answer is fine: type your team name and continue. If it offers to connect a Git repository or start from a template, skip that.
4b · Upload the folder · 2 min
  1. While logged in, go to app.netlify.com/drop. You see a large box saying something like "Drag and drop your project folder here".
  2. Open Finder (Mac) or File Explorer (Windows) beside the browser and find your ai-page folder on the Desktop.
  3. Drag the whole folder (not the file inside it) into the box and let go.
  4. Wait a few seconds. Netlify shows that your project is Published, with an address like https://jolly-pika-4f3a21.netlify.app.
4c · Give the page a clear address · 3 min

The random name tells nobody what the page is about. Change it once, now, and never change it again, because a new address counts as a new page for the robots.

  1. On your project's page in Netlify, click Project configuration in the left menu (on older screens: Site configuration).
  2. Under Project details (or Site details), click Change project name.
  3. Type a short name with words from your question, using only lowercase letters and hyphens, such as instagram-creator-rates-switzerland. If the name is taken, add a word.
  4. Click Save. Your address is now https://instagram-creator-rates-switzerland.netlify.app.
4d · Check that it works · 5 min
  1. Open a private window, paste your new address and press Enter. You should see your page. The address bar now starts with https://, which means it is on the internet.
  2. Open the address on a phone as well, to check that it is readable.
  3. Write the address at the top of your measurement sheet, and paste it below.
  4. Note today's date and time in the sheet as "Version A published".

✅ Step 5 · 20 min · rule A

Tell Google and Bing the page exists

A new page with no links pointing to it can wait a long time before a robot finds it. Two free tools let you tell the robots about it directly and later show whether they have visited. Google's tool feeds Google's AI answers. Bing's tool matters because ChatGPT's web search draws on Bing.

5a · Google Search Console · 12 min
  1. Go to search.google.com/search-console and click Start now. Sign in with your Google account.
  2. You are asked to add a property. Two boxes appear. Use the right-hand box, "URL prefix". Paste your full address, including https:// (your address from Step 4). Click Continue.
  3. Google asks you to prove the site is yours. Under HTML file (the recommended method), click the download button. A small file with a name like google1a2b3c4d5e.html lands in your Downloads folder.
  4. Move that file into your ai-page folder, next to index.html. Don't rename it or open it.
  5. Upload the folder again: in Netlify, open your project, click Deploys, and drag the ai-page folder onto the upload box at the bottom of the page. Wait until the new deploy says Published.
  6. Go back to Search Console and click Verify. You should see "Ownership verified". If it fails, wait one minute and click Verify again.
  7. Click Go to property. In the search bar at the very top ("Inspect any URL"), paste your page's address and press Enter.
  8. It will say "URL is not on Google". That is normal for a new page. Click Request indexing. Wait 1–2 minutes until it says "Indexing requested".
  9. Write Google: indexing requested and the date in the Notes column of your sheet.
5b · Bing Webmaster Tools · 8 min
  1. Go to bing.com/webmasters and click Sign in. Choose Google and use the same Google account as in 5a.
  2. You are offered two ways to add a site. Choose Import from Google Search Console and click Import. Allow Bing to read your Search Console data. Tick your site and click Import. Bing now accepts that the site is yours, so you don't need another verification file.
  3. In the left menu, click URL Submission. Click Submit URLs, paste your page's address and click Submit.
  4. Write Bing: URL submitted and the date in the Notes column of your sheet.
If the import doesn't work, choose "Add your site manually" instead, paste the address, and choose the XML file method. Then repeat steps 3–6 of 5a with Bing's file.
✅ Step 6 · 10 min · rule D

Write the experiment plan

The experiment runs in two phases. Version A stays online without changes until it has been measured for a while. Then you rewrite only the key paragraph (version B) and keep measuring the same way. If the results change after the rewrite, the new writing caused it.

The timeline
  1. Today (week 0): version A goes live. Starting point measured (Step 2).
  2. Weeks 1–3 (phase 1): measure once a week. The page is frozen.
  3. After week 3: if Google Search Console shows "URL is on Google", rewrite the first paragraph only and upload it. This is version B. If Google still hasn't found the page, wait and keep measuring version A. The instructor tells you in which session this happens.
  4. Weeks 4–6 (phase 2): measure once a week, exactly as in phase 1.
  5. Final session: compare phase 1 with phase 2 and present what you learned.
Frozen: never change these during the experiment
  • the page address (the Netlify name)
  • the title and main heading
  • the layout and design
  • any text other than the paragraph you are testing
  • links: don't add links to the page from other sites, and don't share it on social media, in newsletters or in group chats
  • your 3 test questions: same words every time
  • the 3 tools and how you use them: private window, ChatGPT with search turned on
If something goes wrong (for example, the page breaks or someone shares the link), don't hide it. Write it in the Notes column with the date. An honest note keeps the experiment useful.
The weekly check · about 20 minutes, same weekday every week
  1. Repeat Step 2: 3 questions × 3 tools = 9 new rows. Fill in the week number.
  2. If your page is quoted, copy the exact sentence the AI used into Our sentence quoted.
  3. Check rule A: in Search Console, paste your address in the top search bar. Note whether it says "URL is on Google" and the date under Last crawl. In Bing Webmaster Tools, use URL Inspection the same way.
