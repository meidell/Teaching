Session 02 · Week 03 · Thursday 29 October 2026 · 18:45–20:45

Can the board sign off the plan?

Group work, self-run: teams of 2–3 work through this page on their own; the instructor walks around and helps. One laptop per team, signed in to this site. No Excel tonight: the valuation runs on this page.

The mandate. Meridia Cold's board has decided to sell the company. On Monday the data room opens: the confidential folder that the two interested buyers use to prepare their bids. Its key document is management's five-year plan. The board must approve that plan before it goes in, and it has hired your team as its independent reviewer. The CFO's question: "Is this plan credible enough for the board to put its name to? What range of value does it imply? And what is the lowest price we should accept?"

Your deliverable · a one-page plan review memo to Meridia's board, by 20:20

1. Your recommendation: approve the plan, approve it with caveats, or send it back for revision — and why · 2. the value range: what Meridia is worth on management's plan, on the base case and on a lender's case · 3. the two assumptions that decide the price, and how credible each one is · 4. your walk-away price per share, and the assumption it rests on. The page builds the memo from what your team types in Steps 2–5. You put it online as your team's web page on Netlify, and hand in the address. Then, the last 20 minutes: a team quiz, team against team, on tonight's work and on your own memo.

✅ Step 1 · 10 min

The board pack

  1. Read the five facts and the two tables below, as a team.
  2. Type your team's name and members.
  3. Press the button.
The company. Meridia Cold SL, headquartered in Barcelona, stores and transports chilled and frozen goods in fourteen temperature-controlled warehouses in Spain, France and Italy. Most of its revenue comes from multi-year contracts with grocery chains and pharmaceutical distributors. It is capital-intensive (refrigerated buildings and trucks), moderately indebted, and its cash flows are predictable.
What is for sale. 100 % of Meridia's 40.0 million shares — the whole company. A buyer pays the equity value: the enterprise value (what the business is worth) minus Meridia's €60.0 m net debt, which the buyer takes over. Price per share = equity value ÷ 40.0 m.
The two buyers. Boreal Logistics, a larger listed Dutch cold-chain operator: a strategic buyer, which would merge Meridia into its own network, close overlapping warehouses and save costs. Arco Partners, a private-equity fund: a financial buyer, no cost savings, but it would borrow heavily to pay and sell the company again after about five years. Neither has made an offer yet: they will price their bids from the data room.
The plan. Management's five-year plan assumes revenue growth of 9 % a year, an EBITDA margin of 17.5 % from next year, and capex of 8.5 % of revenue. Arco's bank is known to test its own, harsher case before it lends.
Why the board hesitates. If the plan is too optimistic, the buyers' advisers will find out in due diligence, cut their price or walk away, and Arco's bank will not lend. If it is too cautious, the owners sell their company too cheaply. Either way, the board signs the plan.

✅ Step 2 · 20 min · memo part 3

What drives the value?

Management's plan changes four assumptions at once, so nobody can see which one creates the extra value. An independent reviewer separates them: start from the base case, change one assumption to management's figure, read the change in the price per share, and put it back.

  1. Press Base case in the calculator. Note the price per share: €2.48.
  2. Change only revenue growth from 6.7 to 9.0. Read the "vs base case" change under the price per share and type it in the first row below. Press Base case again.
  3. Do the same for the EBITDA margin (16.1 → 17.5), capex (8.0 → 8.5) and working capital (10 → 10: it does not change in the plan, so you should get 0.00).
  4. Press Check on each row. A wrong number turns red: try again.
  5. As a team, write which two assumptions decide Meridia's price, and why the third matters so little.

✅ Step 3 · 20 min · memo parts 1 and 3

Is the plan credible?

A plan is credible when it follows from what the company has actually done, or when management can show what will change. Compare the plan with Meridia's own three years, using the board pack table in Step 1.

  1. Work out Meridia's actual figures for each year and type them in the rows below (one decimal). Press Check on each.
  2. Compare each with management's plan: growth 9.0 %, margin 17.5 %, capex 8.5 %, working capital 10 %.
  3. Work out how much extra EBITDA the plan needs in FY2026 compared with keeping last year's margin.
  4. As a team, write your verdict on the two assumptions from Step 2, and the evidence the board should ask management for before it signs.
How to read itA margin rising from 16.1 % to 17.5 % in a single year is a bigger jump than Meridia has ever made: its best year added about 0.8 points. Capex and working capital point the other way: the plan spends more on capex than Meridia has (cautious) but ties up less working capital than it has (optimistic). An independent reviewer reports both directions.
✅ Step 4 · 10 min · memo part 2

The value range

Each buyer will pick the plan that suits it: Boreal will be shown management's plan, Arco's bank will use its own case. The board needs the whole range.

  1. In the calculator (Step 2), press each of the three plans and type the price per share it gives below. Press Check.
  2. Read "Value after FY2028" for the base case: how much of Meridia's value depends on the years after the plan ends?
  3. Change the WACC from 8.5 to 9.5 on the base case: a buyer with a higher cost of capital pays less. Type the price per share.
☕ Break — five minutes, when your team is ready. Then Step 5.
✅ Step 5 · 10 min · memo part 4

The walk-away price

Before any offer arrives, the board fixes the lowest price per share it will accept, so that it cannot be talked down in the negotiation. There is no single right answer: the number is your team's judgement, and it must rest on an assumption you can defend.

  1. Use the box below: type a price per share and read the EBITDA margin Meridia would need to be worth that much (the other assumptions stay at the base case).
  2. Ask: would we bet that Meridia reaches that margin? Meridia's margin today is 16.1 %.
  3. Agree a walk-away price, and write the assumption it rests on and why the board should not go lower.

✅ Step 6 · 25 min · write, publish, hand in

Write, publish and hand in the memo

6a · The recommendation · 8 min
  1. As a team, decide: should the board approve the plan, approve it with caveats (say which), or send it back for revision (say what must change)?
  2. Write it in the box, with the reason, in five sentences or fewer.
  3. Read the memo preview under it. Anything marked missing is still to do: go back to that step.
6b · Save the memo as a web page · 3 min
  1. On your Desktop, create a folder called meridia-memo.
  2. Press Download the memo. A file called Meridia-plan-review-….html lands in your Downloads folder.
  3. Rename it to exactly index.html and move it into the meridia-memo folder. (Windows: if you cannot see ".html", turn on View → File name extensions in File Explorer first.)
  4. Double-click index.html: your memo opens in the browser. To fix something, change it on this page, download again and replace the file.
6c · Put it online with Netlify · 10 min
  1. Go to app.netlify.com/signup and choose Sign up with Email (or GitHub or Google). Open the confirmation e-mail from Netlify and click the link. If Netlify asks questions about you, any answer is fine; skip any offer to connect a Git repository or use a template. Keep this account: your team uses it again later in the course.
  2. Go to app.netlify.com/drop. Drag the whole meridia-memo folder (not the file inside it) into the large box. After a few seconds Netlify says Published, with a random address like jolly-pika-4f3a21.netlify.app.
  3. Give it a clear address: click Project configuration (older screens: Site configuration) → Change project name, type meridia-review- and your team's name, e.g. meridia-review-team-ebro, and click Save. Lowercase letters and hyphens only; if the name is taken, add a word.
  4. Open the new address in a private window to check it works, then paste it below.
Need to fix it after it is online? Change it here, download again, replace index.html in the folder, then in Netlify open your project → Deploys and drag the folder onto the upload box at the bottom. The address stays the same. The page tells search engines not to list it: it is a memo to a board.
Starts with https:// and ends with .netlify.app.
6d · Hand in · 1 min

The team member who is signed in on this laptop presses Hand in the memo. The memo, the address and the time are saved in their record, with the team's names in it.

✅ Step 7 · 20 min · team quiz

Team quiz: Jeopardy

The last 20 minutes: team against team, on the screen at the front. Six categories, 100 to 500 points. Five are on tonight's work; the sixth, Your memo, asks the team whose turn it is about its own memo — so the team that did the steps scores.

  1. Close the laptops, except one per team: keep your memo open on it (your Netlify address).
  2. Teams take turns choosing a category and a value. The team that chose answers first; one answer per team, agreed in 10 seconds.
  3. A right answer wins the points; a wrong one loses them, and the next team may try.
  4. When the quiz is over, press the button below.
