      Let the computer propose the whole timetable

      Placing classes one at a time means going back every time a later class does not fit, and asking the same teachers again. Here the computer looks at every class of the term at once. It uses three things collected once: the times each teacher marked as available, the students who take each class (from their study plans), and the rooms. It proposes a timetable in which no student of a cohort has two classes at once, no teacher or room is booked twice, and no teacher teaches at a time they marked as not free. You check the proposal, keep what you like, and ask each teacher only once.

          The proposed week of a full-time student

          Each box is one class with its room and teacher. Click a class to read why the computer put it there. Press Pin on a class to keep it where it is for certain. “Solve again” never moves a pinned class, and moves the other classes only when that makes the timetable better. An amber edge marks a class that moved in the last solve.

          Ask each teacher once

          Each teacher receives a draft with only their own classes: the time, the room and the group. They answer once: they accept, or they object and give a reason. Every accepted class is then fixed, and the computer moves only the classes with an objection, to a time the teacher can take. Nobody is asked twice. In this demo the answers are simulated; nothing is sent.
