How an assistant searches the web — and why it invents links

With search switched on, the assistant does four things. It writes queries (“digital marketing internship January 2027”). A search engine returns pages. It reads a few of them. Then it writes its answer, citing those pages. Errors come from each step: an offer page left online after the hiring ended; a site that collects offers and keeps old ones; two pages mixed into one offer.

And when no page fits, the model does what it always does: it writes the most plausible text. An address like company.com/careers/marketing-internship-2027 has exactly the shape of a real link — and does not need to exist to be written. That is why the rule “invent no link” changes the result, and why you open every link yourself.

Where are the real free courses? Often at the makers of the tools themselves — courses from Google, Meta, Microsoft, HubSpot or Canva — and on the big platforms (Coursera, OpenClassrooms), where you can often take the course for free and pay only for the certificate. Conditions change: the course’s own page is what counts, not the agent’s answer.
