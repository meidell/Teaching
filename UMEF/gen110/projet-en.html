Everything happens on this page, in four steps. 1 · Choose one of the six projects. 2 · Say who you do it with. 3 · Fill in hand-in 1, five fields saved as you type. 4 · Follow the plan, session by session, with one box to fill in each week. Step 1 · choose

Six projects to choose from — pick one

Six projects, small, real, verifiable, each with one free tool from the course. You take one, change the names to your own family, shop or friends — and the five fields of hand-in 1 almost write themselves. Click a card to read more, then “I take this project”. You can change your mind until Session 3.

Step 2 · the team

Alone, or with whom?

The project is done alone or in a pair. The list below is the course’s students who are signed in on this site. Click a name to make them your partner: they see it on their page and accept with one click — you then share the same project and plan. Nobody? Click “I work alone”.

Step 3 · hand-in 1 · filled in together in Session 2, Tuesday 20 October, first hour

My project, on half a page

Five fields = hand-in 1

Something AI should do for you.

A real problem from your programme, your student job, a club, your family — anything repetitive, time-consuming, where an AI could help. Small, real, verifiable: at the end we can say whether it worked well or badly. Write in the fields; they save by themselves and I read them from my dashboard. Once all five are filled, hand-in 1 is done.

Step 4 · the path

The work plan, session by session

One block per session, from Session 2 to 12 January: what the session gives you, two or three things to do for your project before the next session, and one box to fill in — two or three sentences, saved, that I read from my dashboard. Hand-ins 2 to 5 fall every two sessions; the block tells you when. Tick what is done.

                      What is graded · January 2027

                      The dossier, the prototype, the presentation

                      (a) The dossier

                      Eight pages at most, one PDF, here, on Friday 15 January 2027 at 23:59.

                      1. The problem — for whom, how it is done today, why it is worth it.
                      2. The data — what you used, where it comes from, what you were not allowed to use.
                      3. The prototype — what you built, with which tools, how it works (screenshots).
                      4. Results and limits — what you measured, where it gets it wrong, and how often.
                      5. Risks and compliance — personal data, costly errors, dependence on a tool, what you never paste into an AI.
                      6. The recommendation — should it be deployed, on what conditions, and what it costs.

                      The dossier ends with an AI declaration: which assistants, for what, and what you checked yourself. In this course, using AI is the point; hiding that you used it is the offence.

                      (b) The prototype

                      In working-demo state, built with the course’s tools.

                      It runs in front of the class on 12 January: a custom assistant, an automation, a site or an app, an AI-driven Excel file, a classifier… It is not uploaded: it is shown. Hand-in 4 is its version 1, hand-in 5 the final version with the presentation plan.

                      (c) The presentation

                      Eight minutes on Tuesday 12 January 2027, plus three of questions.

                      Problem, demo, value, risks — in that order. One grade for the pair, weighted where appropriate by each one’s contribution. The project replaces the midterm exam.
