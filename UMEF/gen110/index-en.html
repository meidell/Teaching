The course · session by session

Ten half-days (Tuesdays 13:30–16:45, two 90-minute blocks) then the final exam. Each card lists what we do together that day and, every other session, the suggested project hand-in. Sessions open as the term goes; changes are announced on the Swiss UMEF platform.

The project · your idea, alone or in a pair

40% · Something AI should do for you

Alone or in a pair, free topic: a real problem from your programme, your student job, a club, your family — anything repetitive, time-consuming, where an AI could help. You build it with the course’s tools (a custom assistant, an automation, a site or an app, an AI-driven Excel file, a classifier…), you measure whether it works, and you say honestly what it is worth, what it risks and what it costs.

(a) The dossierEight pages at most: problem, data, prototype, results and limits, risks and compliance, recommendation. Friday 15 January 2027, 23:59, on the Swiss UMEF platform.
(b) The prototypeIn working-demo state, built with the course’s tools. It runs in front of the class.
(c) The presentationEight minutes on 12 January 2027 + three of questions. Problem, demo, value, risks.

One grade for the pair, weighted where appropriate by each one’s contribution. In line with the regulations, the project replaces the midterm exam.

    10% · Participation, attendance and quizzes

    Participation rewards attendance, the work in the workshops and the results in the end-of-session synchronised quizzes. Quizzes cannot be retaken: a missed quiz counts zero, unless arranged in advance.

    How attendance is recorded. At each session I open a short window and the attendance button at the top of this page turns from grey to red. You press it once, on your own device, in the room.

    ⚠ The syllabus ruleBelow 80% participation, the 10% cannot be earned. Below 50% attendance, no exam and the course has to be retaken. Every absence must be justified; two latenesses count as one absence. Arrived late, flat battery? Tell me — I can mark you by hand.

    50% · Written final exam — Wednesday 3 February 2027, 09:45

    Individual, closed book, two hours, on the whole course: what an AI can and cannot do, how to ask it well, reading a result and checking it, analysing a business use case, its risks and compliance. Session 10 gives the instructions and example questions.

    Preparing for itEach session’s synchronised quiz is in the exam’s style. Redoing them on this site, with the explanations, is the best revision.

    Grading scale

    • A Excellent · 6.00, 5.75, 5.50, 5.25   B Very good · 5.00, 4.75   C Good · 4.50   D Satisfactory · 4.25   E Pass · 4.00   F Fail · < 4.00

    Academic honesty: Swiss UMEF’s code of ethics applies — cite your sources, including when an AI assistant helped you. In this course, using AI is the point; hiding that you used it is the offence.

    Tools and readings

    The tools we use — all in their free version

    • Assistants — ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Le Chat (Mistral), Copilot. At least two accounts, to compare.
    • Sound, image, video — ElevenLabs or your assistant’s voice, Canva, Ideogram or Bing Image Creator, CapCut / Canva for video.
    • Office — Excel and Word (Copilot if your account has it, otherwise the assistant alongside), or Google Sheets and Docs with Gemini.
    • Sites and apps — Lovable, Bolt or your assistant’s canvas; GitHub Pages or Netlify to publish.
    • Automation and agents — Make or Zapier; the assistants’ “GPTs”, “Gems” and “Projects”.
    • No-code machine learning — Teachable Machine ↗.

    Nothing to install: a browser, a Google account and a laptop with a webcam at every session.

    To go further — nothing to buy

    • Elements of AI ↗ — University of Helsinki, free online course with a certificate.
    • Mitchell, M. (2019). Artificial Intelligence: A Guide for Thinking Humans. — the readable reference, no technical background needed.
    • Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 ↗ — the “AI Act”, Session 7.
    • FADP (2023) ↗ and GDPR ↗ — data protection, Session 7.
    • Free certificates worth a CV line: Google “AI Essentials”, Microsoft “AI Skills”, LinkedIn Learning via the library — Session 9.
