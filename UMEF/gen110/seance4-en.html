Agents that work together

What we do today. You learn to make several AIs work together, like a small team. One AI works out a shop’s sales. A second writes the message for the owner. A third checks the numbers. Then you do the same for your project, with your own data.

Why this matters to you. One AI asked to do everything often gets things wrong — and nobody notices. When each AI has one job and another one checks, there are fewer mistakes, and you know where they are. Companies are starting to work this way: knowing how to organise such a team, and when a human must decide, is a skill employers look for. And your project leaves with a first real result.

The situation

Wax & Co, ready-to-wear shop in Sacré-Cœur, Dakar. Eighty orders from July to October, in a file kept by hand. Every Monday, Aminata wants on WhatsApp: the turnover, the top-earning product and its share, October’s rise over July, the average basket, the share of Wave payments. One assistant asked to do everything? It computes, it writes and it checks itself — and nobody verifies. So the work is split as in a real team: an analyst computes, a writer writes, a checker checks, Aminata decides.

The Wax & Co team · three cards

What you just wrote is what ChatGPT calls instructions (in a GPT), Gemini a Gem, Claude a Project: a text the model reads before every message. The model does not change; only what it reads first changes. An “agent team” is therefore the same model, several times, with different cards — and results handed from one to the next.

A good card answers six questions: who are you, what do you do, what do you receive, what do you return, what do you never do, who do you hand on to.

Each agent is right 90% of the time. How many correct reports out of 100, with five agents in a chain?

An error at any link spoils the report

The arithmetic is simple: if each agent is right 9 times in 10, two agents in a row are right 0.9 × 0.9 = 81 times in 100, five agents 59 times. Errors do not cancel out, they pile up. A checker that recomputes from the file catches part of the wrong reports; a checker that only sees the message catches almost nothing — it checks that the text is consistent, not that it is true.
