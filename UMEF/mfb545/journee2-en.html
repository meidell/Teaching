Payments and digital money

A card payment involves five parties in under two seconds; a mobile money withdrawal in Dakar depends on the liquidity of a neighbourhood agent; a CHF 200 transfer costs anything from under 1% to over 10% depending on the channel. Anatomy of a payment

Instructor’s talk. If it does not take place, read this section on your own, then do the sorting exercise at the bottom of the page.

The four-corner model

Five parties behind every card payment

Cardholderyou, with the card
Issuerthe bank that issued the card
SchemeVisa, Mastercard
Acquirerthe merchant’s bank or PSP
Merchantthe shop, the website

“Four corners”: cardholder, issuer, acquirer, merchant. The scheme (card network) sits in the middle: it sets the rules, routes the messages and computes each party’s position. Between the merchant and the acquirer there is often a payment service provider (Stripe, Adyen, Worldline…).

The mechanism · from the card to the merchant’s account

Authorisation, clearing, settlement — eight steps

A coffee paid CHF 4.80 contactless
Who earns what

The merchant discount rate, broken down

Merchant discount rate (MDR) = interchange + scheme fees + acquirer’s margin

On a CHF 100 purchase with a 1.5% MDR, the merchant receives CHF 98.50. The interchange (set by the scheme, paid by the acquirer to the issuer) pays the issuer for the risk and credit it carries; the scheme fees pay Visa or Mastercard; the rest is the acquirer’s margin, which also pays for the technical processing.

  • In the EU, Regulation 2015/751 caps interchange on consumer cards at 0.2% (debit) and 0.3% (credit).
  • In Switzerland, there is no law: agreements with the Competition Commission (COMCO) brought domestic credit-card interchange down to 0.44% on average (since 2017).
  • Interchange is what funds neobanks’ “free” cards: they are issuers, and earn the interchange on every payment.
Security and fraud

Strong customer authentication, 3-D Secure, and who pays for fraud

The European PSD2 directive requires strong customer authentication (SCA) for most online payments: two factors out of knowledge (a code), possession (the phone) and inherence (fingerprint, face). For cards, it goes through 3-D Secure: the issuer asks for confirmation in its app. Version 2 is risk-based: the issuer receives around a hundred data points (device, history, amount) and lets low-risk payments through without friction.

The liability shift: when a payment has been authenticated with 3-D Secure, fraud is borne by the issuer. Without authentication, the cardholder disputes, the issuer raises a chargeback and the merchant loses the sale.

Without a card

Instant payment, TWINT, QR-bill

  • Instant payment. In Switzerland, the SIC system (operated by SIX for the Swiss National Bank) has processed instant payments since August 2024: credited within seconds, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, as ISO 20022 messages. In the EU, Regulation 2024/886 requires banks to receive and then to send SEPA instant transfers (ten seconds at most) at the price of an ordinary transfer, with verification of the payee’s name.
  • TWINT. The app is linked to the user’s bank account; payment is made by scanning a QR code (or showing one), authorisation is given in real time, and the merchant is credited by its acquirer. No card network in the middle: TWINT is its own scheme.
  • The QR-bill. Since 30 September 2022, it has replaced the orange and red payment slips. The QR code contains no money: it contains a pre-filled payment order — IBAN, amount, reference — which the payer’s banking app reads and submits after confirmation.

B · 20 min · the IBAN and the QR-bill

The check that prevents sending money to the wrong account

A Swiss IBAN has 21 characters: CH, two check digits, five institution digits (the bank), twelve account digits. To check it (ISO 13616 standard): move the first four characters to the end, replace each letter with a number (A = 10 … Z = 35; C = 12, H = 17), and divide the resulting large number by 97. An IBAN is valid if the remainder is 1. To compute the check digits, do the same with “00” in their place, and take 98 minus the remainder.

IBAN checker, step by step

The QR reference (27 digits) ends with a check digit computed by “recursive modulo 10”: start with a carry of 0; for each digit, the new carry is entry (carry + digit) mod 10 of the table 0 9 4 6 8 2 7 1 3 5; at the end, the check digit is (10 − carry) mod 10.

Section 2.3 · Block 2
🎤 Talk · 15 min
Mobile money and transfers

  
Instructor’s talk, or self-study reading.


  

    The mechanism · electronic money
    
Where does the money in a Wave or Orange Money wallet come from?

    
    
      
  • One-for-one issuance. When a customer hands 10,000 FCFA in cash to an agent, the electronic money issuer records 10,000 FCFA in their wallet. In return, the issuer holds 10,000 FCFA in a safeguarding account at a bank. The sum of the wallets always equals this account: electronic money is not created, it is converted.
    • 
      
  • The wallet is a claim of the customer on the issuer, not a bank deposit: the issuer cannot lend this money. That is the BCEAO rule in the WAEMU and the rule of Directive 2009/110/EC in the EU.
    • 
      
  • The agent is a neighbourhood shopkeeper who keeps two stocks: cash in the drawer and electronic money (the float) on the phone. A deposit (cash-in) means receiving cash and giving up float; a withdrawal (cash-out), the reverse. The agent earns a commission per transaction.
    • 
      
  • Rebalancing. When the float or the cash runs out, the agent must go to the bank or to a super-agent to convert one into the other — a trip, time, a risk of theft. An agent who runs short turns customers away: that is the first breaking point of a network.
    • 
    

    
The measured effect. In Kenya, access to M-Pesa (launched in 2007) lifted about 2% of households — some 194,000 — out of extreme poverty, with a stronger effect for female-headed households (Suri and Jack, Science, 2016).

  


  

    The mechanism · an international transfer
    
What a transfer really costs: the fees and the rate

    
Total cost = amount sent − (amount received ÷ mid-market rate)

    
The mid-market rate is the midpoint between buy and sell on the foreign-exchange market. A provider advertising “zero fees” often makes its money on the rate. Between the CFA franc and the euro, the rate is fixed: €1 = 655.957 FCFA; so CHF 1 = 655.957 ÷ (price of one euro in francs) FCFA.

    
    
      
  • The bank (SWIFT transfer): the money goes through a chain of correspondent banks, each of which may deduct fees; one to three business days.
    • 
      
  • Wise: the money does not cross the border. Wise receives your francs in its Swiss account and pays the recipient from its local account; flows in both directions offset each other. Mid-market rate, displayed fees.
    • 
      
  • To a mobile wallet: a transfer service credits the recipient’s Wave or Orange Money wallet directly; the recipient cashes out at an agent or pays with it.
    • 
      
  • A stablecoin: francs → USDC on an exchange (fees and spread), transfer on the blockchain (network fees), USDC → FCFA with a local intermediary (spread). Each conversion has its cost (Day 4).
    • 
    

    
United Nations Sustainable Development Goal 10.c: bring the cost of remittances below 3% by 2030. The global average measured by the World Bank is about 6% for a 200-dollar remittance, and sub-Saharan Africa is the most expensive region.

Section 2.6 · Block 3
🎤 Talk · 20 min
APIs and open banking

  
Instructor’s talk, or self-study reading.


  

    What an API is
    
A request, a response — like a form filled in by a program

    
An API (application programming interface) lets one program call another. Payment APIs are almost all REST: the program sends an HTTP request to an address (the endpoint) and receives a response in JSON.


POST https://api.stripe.com/v1/payment_intents          ← method + endpoint
Authorization: Bearer sk_test_…                         ← the secret key
Idempotency-Key: order-2026-1043                        ← duplicate guard
{ "amount": 4990, "currency": "chf" }                       ← the body

200 OK
{ "id": "pi_3Q…", "status": "requires_payment_method", "amount": 4990 }

    
    
      
  • Methods: GET reads, POST creates or triggers, DELETE deletes.
    • 
      
  • Status codes: 200 success · 400 malformed request · 401 not authenticated · 402 payment declined · 404 not found · 5xx server-side failure.
    • 
      
  • Amounts in the smallest unit: CHF 49.90 is written 4990 (centimes) — except for currencies without decimals, such as the CFA franc: 10,000 FCFA is written 10000.
    • 
    

  


  

    What protects
    
Keys, tokens, idempotency, webhooks

    
    
      
  • Keys. The secret key (sk_test_… in test, sk_live_… in production) gives full rights over the account: it stays on the merchant’s server, never in a web page or an app. The publishable key (pk_…) may be in the page; it only allows card data to be transmitted.
    • 
      
  • Tokens. The card number goes directly from the browser to the provider, which returns a token (pm_…) to the merchant. The merchant never sees the card: it falls almost entirely outside the scope of the PCI DSS security standard.
    • 
      
  • Idempotency. If the connection drops during a payment, the program resends the request. With the same Idempotency-Key, the provider returns the result of the first one instead of charging twice.
    • 
      
  • Webhooks. Some results arrive later (3-D Secure confirmation, bank transfer). Instead of asking again and again, the merchant provides an address; the provider calls it when the status changes (payment_intent.succeeded). The call is signed, so that an attacker cannot announce a fake payment.
    • 
    

  


  

    Open banking
    
PSD2: banks must open accounts to authorised third parties

    
Since PSD2 (Directive 2015/2366, applied since 2018), a European bank must give access to payment accounts, with the customer’s consent, to two types of licensed providers:

    
    
      
  • AISP — account information service provider: it reads balances and transactions (budget aggregator, accounting tool, credit scoring).
    • 
      
  • PISP — payment initiation service provider: it triggers a transfer from the customer’s account (paying online by bank transfer, without a card).
    • 
    

    
The customer gives consent in their bank’s app (OAuth 2.0 flow, with strong customer authentication); the third party receives an access token limited in time and in rights, never the password. The APIs follow standards such as the Berlin Group’s NextGenPSD2. Switzerland has no legal obligation: opening up happens through market agreements, notably via SIX’s bLink platform. In the EU, the revision (PSD3 and the Payment Services Regulation) is in the process of adoption.

    The situation
    
An online merchant, its payment provider and a card network

    
Teranga Market, a Geneva online shop (groceries and cosmetics for the West African diaspora), collects CHF 1.2 million a year by card. Its provider, PayLéman, charges it 1.8%. A competitor offers 1.4%. Online fraud reaches 0.4% of sales, above the monitoring threshold of the card network Kora. The three parties meet to set: the MDR, who pays for fraud, and whether 3-D Secure is activated.

    
The rules. The three project teams form three tables; at each table, one person per role (three per table). Read only your own card. 20 minutes of negotiation; a deal is recorded in the calculator in the next section. No deal is a possible outcome — to be compared with your fallback option.

    

      
🛒 The merchant
Teranga Market

      
🏦 The provider

🌐 The network

Confidential card · merchant
Teranga Market

    • 
      
  • Today: MDR 1.8% (CHF 21,600) and fraud 0.4% borne entirely by you (CHF 4,800) — CHF 26,400 a year.
    •

    •

    • 
      
  • Your objective: a total payment cost (MDR + fraud + lost sales) below CHF 21,000 a year.

    
Confidential card · provider
PayLéman SA
    
      
  • Your costs: interchange and scheme fees ≈ 0.65% on average, processing 0.20% — 0.85% in total. At 1.8%, this customer earns you CHF 11,400 a year.
    • 
      
  • The network charges you a penalty of 0.10% of volume (CHF 1,200) as long as this merchant’s fraud exceeds 0.3%.
    • 
      
  • You can offer to integrate 3-D Secure (it costs you CHF 1,000 once).
    • 
      
  • Losing the customer: CHF 0.
    • 
      
  • Your objective: keep the customer with at least CHF 4,800 of profit a year, and get 3-D Secure activated.

    
Confidential card · network
Kora
    
      
  • Your fees: 0.10% of volume (CHF 1,200 a year on this merchant), included in the provider’s 0.65%.
    • 
      
  • You cannot change the interchange for a single merchant: it is set for the whole network.
    • 
      
  • You can offer the provider a rebate of 0.05% (CHF 600 a year) if 3-D Secure is activated.
    • 
      
  • An unhappy merchant can push its customers towards TWINT or instant payments — which do not go through you.
    • 
      
  • Your objective: 3-D Secure activated, fraud below 0.3%, and the volume stays on your network.
